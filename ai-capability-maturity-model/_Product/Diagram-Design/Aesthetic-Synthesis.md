[https://protocolized.summerofprotocols.com/](https://protocolized.summerofprotocols.com/).

print/poster artifacts that need to sit comfortably alongside the substack.

## Palette

The substack's recent covers run hot. Saturated, almost lurid color is the rule —
muted/desaturated palettes are not used.

**Recurring colors** (with hex approximations the diagrams should use):

| Role                | Hex        | Where it shows up                                      |
|---------------------|------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Deep ultramarine    | `#0d0a1f`  | Black-blue grounds (#01, #03, #07)                     |
| Cobalt blue         | `#1d3fc4`  | Pyramid interior (#07), molecular mesh (#06), water    |
| Cobalt-light        | `#3f6ae0`  | Glints, ripples, accents                               |
| Amber / gold        | `#e6a73c`  | Glow halos (#07, #12), city lights (#11), accents      |
| Rust / orange-red   | `#c44a1f`  | Sky-flares (#12), cell-edges (#06), warm grounds       |
| Magenta             | `#c7398b`  | Aura tints (#03, #07), splatters (#11)                 |
| Lime / acid green   | `#a8d439`  | Reeds, organic moss accents (#09, #10, #11)            |
| Violet / wine       | `#6b3fb5`  | Mid-tones in foliage (#04), shadow plums (#10)         |
| Teal                | `#2b8a8a`  | Cellular detail (#06, #09)                             |
| Bone / off-white    | `#f4f1e6`  | Bezel rings, outline lines, vertex circles, mist       |
| Ink                 | `#0a0810`  | Heavy outline strokes; "comic" black                   |

**Combination rule**: complementary or near-complementary jumps over saturated
ground. Yellow-on-blue (#07, #12), orange-on-blue (#06), magenta-on-cobalt (#03),
purple-on-bone (#10) are all used. Avoid analogous-only schemes — there's almost
always a hot/cold contrast.

## Composition

- **Centered, symbolic, single hero glyph.** A triangle (#07), a ring (#02, #04, #10),
  an obelisk (#12), a fruit cross-section (#10), a city silhouette (#11). One subject
  per image, dead-center or near-center.
- **Halo behind the subject.** A radial gradient — usually amber or amber→magenta —
  bleeds out of the subject into the deep ground. Acts as both atmospheric depth
  and emphasis cue.
- **Inset framing devices.** When a ring is used (#02, #04, #10), it is bone-white,
  thick, and has small circular bolt-cutouts on the inner edge — reading as an
  industrial/instrument bezel. Keep this exact treatment when borrowing the motif.
- **Square or wide-rectangular crop**, typically 1129×1129 for square posts, 1200×630
  for header crops. Compositions are designed to survive both crops.

## Iconography

Recurring symbolic elements that compose the visual vocabulary:

1. **Triangle** with thin black outline and small open circles at each vertex (#07).
   The vertex-circle is the substack's geometric "signature" — a beat that says
   "abstract diagram, not just illustration."
2. **Bone-white bezel ring** with knurled bolt-port cutouts on the inner edge
   (#02, #04, #10). Frames a textured field; reads as a porthole into a world.
3. **Obelisk / monolith** silhouette against a sunset sky (#12).
4. **Cellular / molecular blob clusters** — heavy-outlined organic shapes joined
   by tendrils (#06, #09).
5. **Cross-sectional fruit/biology** with seeds and structure (#10).
6. **Architectural collage** — building cutouts pasted onto a painterly ground (#11).
7. **Point clouds / dithered marks** for "swarm" or "field" effect (#03 — literal
   point-cloud rendering of figures; backgrounds across most images use noise).

## Treatment / technique

The substack covers feel **mixed-media** — photographic, painterly, and
diagrammatic registers all overlap. Concrete techniques worth replicating:

- **Posterization / risograph color blocks.** Solid, slightly off-register fills
  separated by heavy black outline (#06, #07, #11, #12).
- **Hand-drawn ink linework.** Outlines are not perfectly geometric; they wobble
  slightly. Suggests illustration, not CAD.
- **Heavy black outline (1.5–2.5 px) around primary subjects.** Mandatory when
  the subject sits over a textured ground.
- **Photographic textures behind the icon** — foliage (#04), fruit flesh (#10),
  sky atmosphere (#12). Often heavily color-graded.
- **Glow / aura.** Soft Gaussian-blurred radial gradients sit *under* the icon,
  not over it. Magnitude: large (subject-radius × 1.5 to × 3).
- **Mist / particle swarm** for dissolution effects (#03).
- **Dithering / noise** in flat areas — never plain RGB fills; everything has grain.

## What to do (when producing diagrams in this style)

1. **Single hero icon, centered, with a halo behind it.** Don't fragment.
2. **Bone-white outline + ink-black underline** on the icon edge. Vertex circles.
3. **Saturated complementary palette.** Pick two hot/cold colors per visual; let
   them compete.
4. **Add grain.** Even on flat fills. Use SVG noise filters or subtle radial
   gradients to break up plain RGB.
5. **Use mono type for labels and serif for titles.** JetBrains/Roboto Mono for
   level numerals, axis tics, eyebrows. Cormorant Garamond (or similar literary
   serif) for headlines. This matches both the substack's title face and the
   protocolized.dev site system.
6. **Reserve bezel rings for the "porthole" treatment** — diagrams that look
   *into* a system rather than *at* it.

## What NOT to do

- **No flat infographic look.** No corporate gradient ramps, no rainbow steps,
  no soft-shadow depth tricks. The substack covers are not infographics; they're
  cover art that happens to be diagrammatic.
- **No icons-from-Heroicons.** All icons here are bespoke, hand-drawn, or
  collaged. Borrowed icon sets read as alien immediately.
- **No 3D perspective tricks** unless the subject is genuinely architectural
  (#11, #12). Default to flat / cross-sectional.
- **No skeuomorphic textures** (paper, leather). Textures should be photographic
  *of the subject matter* (water, foliage, fruit) or painterly.
- **No bright #FFFFFF.** Always bone (`#f4f1e6`) for "white" — keeps unity with
  the warmer ink and amber.

## Type system (recommended)

| Element                      | Face                              | Usage                                                      |
|------------------------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Headline / page title        | Cormorant Garamond, 500           | Like the substack post titles — italic emphasis allowed    |
| Lede / pull quote            | Cormorant Garamond italic, 400    | Below headline, max-width ~780 px                          |
| Body                         | Inter, 400                        | Reading copy in panels, cards                              |
| Eyebrow / monospace label    | JetBrains Mono, 400–700 caps      | "DIAGRAM CONCEPT · v1", level numerals, axis labels        |
| In-diagram numerals          | JetBrains Mono, 11 px, +1.5 ls    | "L1 · BASE", "L5 · APEX", crosshair frame IDs              |

## Per-image notes (for future reference)

- **#01 Headless Empire** — Twin obelisks, glitched/dithered, framing a portal.
  Lesson: heavy data-noise can still resolve into a clear silhouette if the
  silhouette is bold.
- **#02 Primordial Soup** — White bezel ring with bolt-cutouts over a blue
  marbled fluid field. Canonical "porthole" treatment.
- **#03 Flesh Perfected** — Two point-cloud figures (one cool/one warm) on
  black. Lesson: dithering as figuration.
- **#04 Fabric and the Brain** — Bezel ring framing a foliage field. Same
  porthole device, different content.
- **#05 Faithful Channel** — Painterly fish/serpent with rainbow gradient
  scales, ink outline, on cream ground. Lesson: rare use of light ground —
  works because the subject is high-contrast.
- **#06 Government Guide** — Cellular molecular forms on cobalt-orange
  gradient. Lesson: orange-on-blue with heavy ink outline survives at thumbnail
  scale.
- **#07 Strangeness, Legibility, Hardness** — **The most diagrammatic of the
  set.** Triangle outline with vertex circles, on a yellow → blue gradient, with
  particle swarm inside the triangle. This is the single most direct reference
  for our pyramid diagrams.
- **#08 Last-Mile Optimism** — Pencil-line landscape with a glowing green
  monolith / sign. Lesson: hand-drawn line + single saturated accent.
- **#09 Atomic Questions** — Cellular blob with branching tendrils, in pink
  and teal. Lesson: protocol-as-organism imagery.
- **#10 Factory to Factory** — Bezel ring around a fruit cross-section. Same
  porthole device. Lesson: biology as protocol metaphor.
- **#11 Caduceus City** — Architectural collage in saturated purple/yellow/
  magenta. Lesson: city silhouettes can carry maturity-level metaphors.
- **#12 American Skyway** — Obelisk against a red-orange sunset sky over a
  fence-line landscape. Lesson: a single iconic vertical against a horizontal
  ground line + halo glow = production-grade hero in minimal elements.

## Application to current diagram concepts

`Watershed-Pyramid-v1.html` and `Pyramid-on-Path-v1.html` (Diagram-Design/)
both apply this synthesis:

- Deep ultramarine ground (`--bg-deep`)
- Bone-white pyramid silhouette (`--bone`)
- Vertex circles at base corners
- Dashed-fade apex (the "disintegrating tip" reading)
- Amber + magenta + cobalt halo behind the pyramid
- JetBrains Mono level numerals, Cormorant Garamond headline
- Crosshair frame IDs in corners (mono, soft-bone)
- Mist particle animations at the apex
