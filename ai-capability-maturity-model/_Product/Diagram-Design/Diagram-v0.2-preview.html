01Iceberg with dissolving tip

▲ with waterline + mist Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★
single iceberg, tip dissolving into mist — L5 becomes the atmosphere

The story it tells

The visible thing is a small fraction of what's there. The very tip is dissolving — at L5, the structure recedes into the surrounding atmosphere. You can't quite tell where the iceberg ends and the sky begins.

How to read it

Bottom up: the L1 base is the largest hidden mass, narrowing as you rise above the waterline at L3. By L5, the iceberg has thinned to a pale gradient with mist particles — visible to anyone, but dissolving into the medium.

L5

dissolving into atmosphere

Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Cultural carry of "tip of the iceberg" does free work
  • The dissolving tip captures L5's structure-becomes-background better than any pyramid version
  • Single-shape recognition; reduces cleanly to a glyph

Weaknesses

  • Vertical-only — misses L4's lateral / sectoral nature
  • Static: icebergs don't grow, so "movement through levels" needs other framing
Mnemonic glyph: triangle + waterline + mist particles

02The Path — single trail, five characters

⌒═ continuous evolving path Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★★
single landscape, single path — character morphs left to right; L5 weaves back into terrain

The story it tells

One path crosses the landscape. Its character changes — not a sequence of separate paths but a single trail evolving. By L5, the engineered road has dissolved back into the terrain it once cut through, becoming part of the ground rather than something laid on top.

How to read it

Read left to right. Cow-path with multiple desire lines (organic, organic) → marked trail with signs (sanctioned) → stone slabs (built, deliberate) → smooth asphalt with center line (engineered, named) → road fading, grass growing through, ghost-trace continuing (the network has become the terrain).

L3

L5

Asphalt fades. Grass grows through. Road becomes the landscape — terrain is now defined by the network's existence.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • This is the smooth-to-striated thesis from Rao's Evil Twin, made literal
  • The L5 "weave back into terrain" captures the model's most subtle claim: at planetary scale, infrastructure stops being separable from ground
  • Single horizontal composition reads in one glance
  • Each transition is visually distinct without any gap

Weaknesses

  • Wide aspect ratio — needs landscape format; doesn't reduce to a square mnemonic glyph as cleanly
  • L5's "weaving back" is a sophisticated claim that needs the legend to read correctly first time

When to use it

Hero diagram for the litepaper / artifact. Tells the model's most important story. Harder as a tiny icon (the mnemonic is the path-shape transition itself).

Mnemonic glyph: path striating then dissolving

03Canyon strata — single cross-section

▤ exposed rock face Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★
single cliff cross-section — all five strata visible at once

The story it tells

Pressure and time consolidate loose material into structure. A single cliff face shows you all five layers at once — you don't need separate diagrams; the geology is one continuous fact, exposed by erosion.

How to read it

Atmosphere at top (L5 — pervasive, surrounding). Surface dust (L1, loose). Sediment beginning to layer (L2). Compacted sandstone (L3). Foundational bedrock at the bottom (L4 — load-bearing). Higher in the cliff = newer; deeper = older and more consolidated.

L1
Shadow

Unconsolidated. Wind-blown. Each particle a separate AI use.
L2
Sanctioned

Layers form. Partial compaction. Beginning structure.
L3
Designed
sandstone
Pressure + time compressed sediment. Hard, navigable.
L4
Infrastructural
bedrock
Foundation. Other things are built on it. Hard to remove.
L5
Planetary
atmosphere
Different kind of structure — pervasive, surrounding, not "above."
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Pressure-and-time framing aligns with the SIG paper
  • Atmosphere as L5 captures medium-not-layer property
  • Single cross-section composition; no panels

Weaknesses

  • Geology readers will note the inversion — newer layers usually atop older — but L1-as-surface still works as a visual
  • L4 "bedrock" reads as foundational (correct) but visually below L3, which can read as "lower = lesser" if you don't get the geology
Mnemonic glyph: cliff-face strata

04Fault line progression — single landscape

↯ stress-revealing structure Iconography ★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★★
single horizon under stress — one continuous earth-line, fracturing left to right

The story it tells

Stress reveals structure. A single horizon under increasing pressure: smooth at first, then cracking, then mapped, then fully tectonic. By L5, you've zoomed out — the cracks you started with are now continental plates seen from orbit.

How to read it

Read left to right as a single landscape under accumulating stress. The horizon line is unbroken at L1 (smooth surface). Cracks emerge at L2. The fault is identified and named at L3. Multiple fault systems form at L4. At L5, the perspective shifts to a planetary view — the fault lines are now plate boundaries.

L3

Org has acknowledged the fault and built protocols around it.
L4
Infrastructural
tectonic structure
Sector-wide fault systems; multiple orgs share the same plates.
L5
Planetary
continental plates
Continental tectonics. Movement is planetary.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Direct site-naming alignment — Finding Fault Lines
  • "Stress reveals structure" matches the model's core thesis
  • Single horizontal composition reads naturally left-to-right
  • The L4→L5 perspective shift (zoom out from cracks to plates) is novel and accurate

Weaknesses

  • "Fault" carries negative connotation in everyday English
  • The L5 perspective shift (close-up to planetary) is conceptual; needs the legend
Mnemonic glyph: cracks → plates

The story it tells

How to read it

Top to bottom: scattered springs in mountains (L1) → creeks merging (L2) → defined river with banks (L3) → river basin spreading into delta (L4) → ocean (L5). Every drop visible at any level passes through every other.

06Concentric transit — zoom from person to planet

◎ nested rings of scale Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★
nested concentric scales — one map zooming out from individual to planetary

The story it tells

Each scale of coordination encloses the previous. Walking happens inside a bus route, which happens inside a metro, which happens inside an intercity network, which happens inside the planetary aviation graph. You don't replace earlier scales — you nest within them.

How to read it

From the center outward: a person walking → a bus route in their neighborhood → a city's metro → regional rail → global aviation network. Each ring is the prior scale embedded in larger coordination.

  • Single map; no panels

Weaknesses

  • Less recognizable as a single iconic shape
  • Reading order (inside-out) is opposite of most maturity models
Mnemonic glyph: nested concentric circles

08Coastline of beacons — single shore, satellite above

The story it tells

Visibility is non-monotonic. The lighthouse is the most visible signal you've ever seen. GPS — which everyone depends on — is invisible. Civilization-scale infrastructure recedes precisely because everyone uses it.

Read along the coast left to right: signal fire (ad hoc) → oil lamp (sanctioned, local) → lighthouse (engineered, named) → lighthouse network (coordinated coastline). Above the entire scene: a GPS satellite, faintly orbiting — the L5 layer that everything below depends on without seeing.

Bottom up: solid foundation (L1), narrowing through Sanctioned, Designed, and Infrastructural. The L4 block is the highest fully-solid region. Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Strongest cultural carry — pyramid is the universal maturity-model glyph
  • The disintegrating tip is a small change with big semantic impact: rejects the "climb to apex" narrative without abandoning the visual familiarity
  • Lowest cognitive cost — readers know what a pyramid means, then notice the twist
  • Reduces cleanly to a tiny glyph (triangle with dotted top)

Weaknesses

  • Still pyramid-shaped — risks being read as classical maturity model by someone who doesn't notice the dissolving tip
  • Doesn't capture L4's lateral / sectoral nature — it's still vertical
  • Some audiences will see "pyramid" and stop reading; the dissolution is subtle

When to use it

Best as a transition diagram if we're keeping the pyramid as canonical. The dissolution tip lets us evolve the existing model without retraining the audience. Pair with the level descriptions to make the L5 framing explicit.

Mnemonic glyph: pyramid + dissolving apex

v0.2 framing

The integrated single-visual treatment matters because the model isn't 5 stages — it's one phenomenon (organizational AI adoption) with five characters along its arc. Disconnected panels imply the levels are separate worlds; integrated visuals show that they're the same terrain at different times / different scales.

Strongest protocol-theory alignment

02 · Path. Cow-path → marked → stone → road → terrain. The smooth-to-striated thesis as one continuous illustration.

Strongest cultural mnemonic

11 · Pyramid revised. Existing visual vocabulary, twisted at the top. Lowest cognitive cost; biggest semantic impact.

Strongest L5-property capture

08 · Coastline of beacons. Visible signals on the shore + invisible satellite above. Captures non-monotonic visibility.

Strongest "stress reveals structure"

04 · Fault progression. Single horizon under accumulating stress — site-naming alignment with Finding Fault Lines.

My pick of the 11: 02 · Path as the hero diagram for the litepaper / artifact (tells the story most accurately), with 11 · Pyramid revised as the small-format mnemonic on result cards / level badges (cultural carry + tiny-glyph readability).
