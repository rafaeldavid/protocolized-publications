01The Iceberg

▲ with waterline Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★ · Protocol ★★
↑ visible to most · ↓ submerged majority

The story it tells

The visible thing is a small fraction of what's there. "Tip of the iceberg" is already the cultural mnemonic for the L1 Shadow problem. L5 sits at the very tip — visible but receding, infrastructure that becomes background.

How to read this diagram

L1 Shadow is the submerged base — the hidden mass everyone has, nobody talks about. L2–L4 progressively emerge above the waterline. L5 is the visible tip — paradoxically, the most-pervasive layer is also the one that recedes from notice once everyone depends on it.

L1
Shadow
submerged base
Hidden mass; everyone's there but nobody admits it. Largest single-level population.
L2
Sanctioned
just at waterline
Just-emerged, partially visible. Mandate exists; protocol doesn't.
L3
Designed
just above water
Visible, defended, but minority. Designed workflows with named owners.
L4
Infrastructural
tip just above
Few orgs reach here; sectoral coordination.
L5
Planetary
highest peak — recedes
Visible to all; also a different kind of invisible — civilization-scale infrastructure.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Memorable shape; "tip of the iceberg" is in cultural vocabulary already
  • Communicates that visible activity is a small fraction of total
  • Implies hidden mass doesn't go away just because you don't see it

Weaknesses

  • Static — icebergs don't grow; doesn't show how an org moves through levels
  • Vertical-only; misses L4's lateral / sectoral nature
  • L5's "infrastructure-becomes-invisible" inverts the iceberg's logic of "visible = above water"

When to use it

Best for opening framing — the first encounter with the model, when the priority is "you are not where you think you are." Cultural carry of "tip of the iceberg" does free work.

Mnemonic glyph: triangle bisected by waterline

02Desire path → highway

⌒→═ evolving path Iconography ★★★ · Story ★★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★★
smooth → striated (Rao, Evil Twin)

The story it tells

This is the protocol-theory thesis: protocols turn smooth behavior spaces into striated ones. The terrain doesn't change — the structure imposed on it does. Each level is a different degree of striation.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: open terrain (people moving freely) → emergent track (worn by repeated use) → marked trail (signed) → engineered road (paved, named) → highway network (multi-route, sector-spanning). The terrain at L5 is now defined by the network, not the network by the terrain.

L1
Shadow
open terrain
No path. Anyone goes anywhere. Pure smoothness.
L2
Sanctioned
desire path
Worn into being by repeated use. Acknowledged, not designed.
L3
Designed
marked trail
Someone signed it. Maintained. Still single-org.
L4
Infrastructural
paved road
Engineered. Named. Cross-org coordination.
L5
Planetary
highway network
Multi-route, sector-spanning. Terrain defined by the network now.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Direct quote anchor in Rao's Constructing the Evil Twin of AI — protocols turn smooth behavior spaces into striated ones
  • Captures cumulative agency: each layer is a deliberate addition of structure, not "more of the same"
  • Non-monotonic visibility — the highway exists because it was built, but the road that's been there 100 years recedes into infrastructure

Weaknesses

  • Glyph isn't iconic the way pyramid / iceberg are — it's a process more than a shape
  • Five-level distinction is clean but each individual glyph is plain

When to use it

Best for SIG-internal / protocol-theory-aware audiences. The metaphor does heavy work for anyone who's read Evil Twin; less so for cold readers.

Mnemonic glyph: 5 stages of path striation

03Geological strata

▤ stacked sediment Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★
pressure + time compresses each layer

The story it tells

Pressure and time consolidate loose material into structure. Each layer is a different category of substance, not just "more of the same." The protocol-theory framing lifts directly from Finding Fault Lines within the Firm.

How to read this diagram

Bottom to top: loose dust (individual AI use) → sediment (sanctioned, layered but loose) → sandstone (designed, hard, navigable) → bedrock (foundational, sector-wide) → atmosphere (surrounding, pervasive). L5 is structurally different — it's the medium, not a layer.

L1
Shadow
loose dust / gravel
Unconsolidated. Wind-blown. Each particle a separate AI use.
L2
Sanctioned
sediment
Beginning to settle. Layers form, but pressure hasn't compacted them.
L3
Designed
sandstone
Pressure + time compressed sediment into structure. Hard, navigable.
L4
Infrastructural
bedrock
Foundation. Other things are built on it. Hard to remove.
L5
Planetary
atmosphere
Different kind of structure — pervasive, surrounding, not "above."
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Pressure-and-time framing aligns with how the SIG paper treats organizational protocols — accumulated stress reveals underlying structure
  • Each level is a literal different material type — visual distinction is sharp
  • L5 as "atmosphere" captures the L5-as-medium framing that pyramids miss

Weaknesses

  • Inverts traditional "high = good" reading — older / deeper layers are usually more foundational geologically
  • L5 doesn't tile cleanly with the other layers (it's qualitatively different)

When to use it

Best for the long-form artifact / litepaper context where the reader has time to absorb the metaphor. Less effective as a small mnemonic.

Mnemonic glyph: five stacked strata

04Fault line cross-section

↯ stress reveals structure Iconography ★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★★★ (site naming)
Finding Fault Lines — stress reveals structure

The story it tells

Protocols are responses to pressure, not arbitrary construction. Smooth surface accumulates stress invisibly until something cracks. Direct site-naming alignment — finding-fault-lines.jpg is the hero image across all six pages.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: smooth surface → first cracks emerge under stress → fault is mapped, named, and worked with → multi-fault tectonic structure (sectoral) → planetary plates (civilization-scale).

L1
Shadow
smooth surface
Pressure is real but invisible; nothing has cracked yet.
L2
Sanctioned
first cracks
Stress finds the weak places. Cracks visible but unmapped.
L3
Designed
fault mapped + named
Org has acknowledged the fault and built protocols around it.
L4
Infrastructural
tectonic structure
Sector-wide fault systems; multiple orgs share the same plates.
L5
Planetary
civilization-scale plates
Continental tectonics. Movement is planetary.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Direct site-naming alignment — Finding Fault Lines within the Firm is the SIG foundation paper
  • "Stress reveals structure" matches the model's core thesis about why protocols exist
  • Captures protocols as response, not imposition

Weaknesses

  • Less universally recognized than pyramid / iceberg — needs explanation
  • Glyph is harder to reduce — fault lines are jagged and irregular
  • "Fault" carries negative connotation in everyday English (this is "your fault")

When to use it

Best for SIG-internal contexts and the litepaper. Strong narrative fit with the existing site identity.

Mnemonic glyph: jagged fault traces

05River / watershed system

⌇ branching flow Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★ · Protocol ★★★
individual sources → planetary convergence

The story it tells

Many sources converge into shared infrastructure. Each level is a different stage of consolidation. Convergent rather than additive.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: springs (individual, scattered) → creeks (first merging) → river (one channel, defined banks) → river basin (multiple rivers, sectoral) → ocean (planetary convergence).

L1
Shadow
springs / drips
Individual sources. No coordination.
L2
Sanctioned
creeks
First merging. Visible flow but unstable banks.
L3
Designed
river with channel
Banks defined. Flow predictable.
L4
Infrastructural
river basin
Multiple rivers, shared watershed. Sector.
L5
Planetary
ocean
Convergence into one body. Planetary scale.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Universal cultural metaphor — readers know what watersheds do
  • Convergence framing fits the model's L4 / L5 sector dynamics
  • Directionality (downstream) implies adoption flow

Weaknesses

  • "Flowing downhill" can read as decline rather than maturation
  • Implies rivers and oceans are inevitable destinations — most orgs never reach L4 / L5
  • L1 as "springs" is too neutral / pretty; the model's L1 has danger

When to use it

Best for the litepaper macro-framing of sector-wide adoption (L4 / L5 discussion).

Mnemonic glyph: branching flow

06Subway / transit network

●─● nodes + edges Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★
5 zoom levels of transit coordination

The story it tells

Coordination scope expands at each level. Each scale (individual / route / network / region / planet) is structurally different.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: walking (each person picks their path) → single bus route → designed metro → multi-city rail mesh → global aviation hubs. Each scale needs different protocols (and different tooling) to function.

L1
Shadow
walking
Each person makes their own path.
L2
Sanctioned
bus route
Single line, sanctioned, but rough.
L3
Designed
subway
Engineered, multiple stops, one city.
L4
Infrastructural
intercity rail
Multiple cities, shared standards.
L5
Planetary
aviation network
Every node reachable through hubs.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Coordination scope is immediately legible
  • Each scale (individual / city / region / planet) is structurally distinct
  • Maps cleanly to the model's "what kind of governance does this scale need?" question

Weaknesses

  • Glyph is busy — five different network shapes don't reduce to one icon
  • Scale jumps are large; small-org users may not relate to "intercity rail"

When to use it

Best when the audience already understands the model's levels and wants to see scale made vivid. Less effective as the introductory diagram.

Mnemonic glyph: nodes connected by edges

07Coral reef succession

🪸 ecological emergence Iconography ★★ · Story ★★★ · Protocol ★★
ecological succession — bottom-up emergence

The story it tells

Living systems build themselves. Each layer enables the next. Cooperation between organisms creates structure no single organism could.

How to read this diagram

Bottom to top: bare seafloor (no structure) → pioneer corals (early settlers) → established reef (biodiverse, structured) → barrier reef (sectoral, multi-species) → ocean ecology (planetary currents and climate).

L1
Shadow
bare seafloor
No structure. Substrate exposed.
L2
Sanctioned
pioneer corals
Early settlers. Beginning of structure.
L3
Designed
established reef
Biodiverse, structured ecosystem.
L4
Infrastructural
barrier reef
Sectoral, multi-species cooperation.
L5
Planetary
ocean ecology
Currents, migration, climate-scale.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Captures emergent organization and inter-organism cooperation
  • Beautiful, distinctive visual language
  • Bottom-up framing matches how protocols actually develop

Weaknesses

  • Niche metaphor — coral reefs aren't universally understood
  • Hard to draw at small scale and remain legible
  • "Pretty" risks undermining the model's seriousness for an enterprise audience

When to use it

Best for sustainability-adjacent or biological-systems audiences. Probably not for a deployment-manager-facing artifact.

Mnemonic glyph: stacked organic structures

08Lighthouse → beacon network

⛯ tower + rays Iconography ★★★ · Story ★★★★ · Protocol ★★★
signal infrastructure: visible → invisible

The story it tells

Visibility is non-monotonic. A lighthouse is the most visible signal. GPS — which everyone depends on — is invisible. Civilization-scale infrastructure recedes precisely because everyone uses it.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: signal fire (ad hoc) → oil lamp (sanctioned, local) → lighthouse (engineered, named) → lighthouse network (coordinated coastline) → GPS / satellite (planetary, invisible to most).

L1
Shadow
signal fire
Ad hoc; whoever has wood lights one.
L2
Sanctioned
oil lamp
Standardized fuel, fixed location, local responsibility.
L3
Designed
lighthouse
Engineered, characteristic light pattern, named.
L4
Infrastructural
lighthouse network
Coordinated along coastlines; ships expect it.
L5
Planetary
GPS / satellite
Invisible to most; civilization depends on it.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Maps L5's "infrastructure-becomes-invisible" framing better than any other analogy here
  • Visibility is non-monotonic — handles the L3-most-visible / L5-least-visible inversion
  • "Lighthouse" carries clear cultural meaning (signal, navigation, safety)

Weaknesses

  • L1's "signal fire" reads as primitive, when AI L1 is actually modern shadow tools
  • Glyph reduction is medium — tower with rays is recognizable but not unique

When to use it

Best when the audience needs to understand the L4 → L5 transition specifically. The lighthouse-to-GPS arc captures it cleanly.

Mnemonic glyph: tower with rays

09Protocol stack (OSI-style)

▤ layered rectangles Iconography ★★★★ · Story ★★★ · Protocol ★★★★
cumulative dependency, OSI-style

The story it tells

Each level is a layer that depends on the one below. The model literally is a protocol stack — this metaphor doesn't translate, it's identity.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: civilization-scale coordination → sector-wide standards → org-specific designed workflows → organizational permission + access → individual unsanctioned use. Each layer needs the one below to function.

L1
Shadow
individual layer
Raw use of tools by individuals; no abstraction.
L2
Sanctioned
access layer
Connection sanctioned; flow controlled.
L3
Designed
workflow layer
Routing, addressing, reliable delivery within an org.
L4
Infrastructural
application layer
HTTP / DNS / SSL — sector-wide standards.
L5
Planetary
civilization layer
The Web — civilization-scale coordination layer.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Most directly protocol-theory native — the model literally is a protocol stack
  • Each layer's dependency on the lower one is built in
  • Glyph reduces cleanly to stacked rectangles

Weaknesses

  • Reads as technical — non-engineer audiences may bounce
  • Visually it's a pyramid on its side (or close to it)
  • Doesn't capture the "smooth → striated" dynamism that makes the model not-just-static

When to use it

Best for technical audiences (engineering managers, platform leads). Good as the "how does this map to what I already know?" diagram for OSI-literate readers.

Mnemonic glyph: five stacked layers

10Construction site → city

🏛 tangible scalar Iconography ★★★ · Story ★★★ · Protocol ★★
tangible scalar — built environment

The story it tells

Each level is a different category of built thing. Tents, houses, designed buildings, city blocks, and smart cities aren't degrees of "more building" — they're different kinds of structure with different governance.

How to read this diagram

Top to bottom: tent (improvised) → house (built informally) → designed building (architect-led) → city block (zoning + utilities) → smart city / planetary infrastructure.

L1
Shadow
tent
Improvised, individual.
L2
Sanctioned
house
Built but informal; no shared code.
L3
Designed
architected building
Plans, codes, engineer-stamped.
L4
Infrastructural
city block
Zoning, utilities, shared streets.
L5
Planetary
smart city
Embedded sensors, coordination at scale.
Strengths · weaknesses · when to use

Strengths

  • Tangible — readers can picture each stage
  • Each stage clearly more complex than the prior
  • Maps to real-world governance categories (zoning, building codes, utilities)

Weaknesses

  • Implies "bigger = better" which the model contests for L4 / L5
  • Generic — many maturity models use construction as a metaphor
  • "Planetary smart city" is harder to picture than the other stages

When to use it

Best for non-technical audiences who need a concrete metaphor. The construction frame does well in conversation; less in print.

Mnemonic glyph: buildings of increasing scale

Recommendation framing

Three contenders if we want to leave the pyramid behind. The fourth option is to keep the pyramid as the canonical glyph and use one of these as a secondary diagram alongside the level descriptions.

Strongest protocol-theory alignment

02 · Desire path → highway. This is the smooth-to-striated thesis. Direct anchor in Rao's Evil Twin.

Strongest cultural mnemonic

01 · Iceberg. "Tip of the iceberg" is already in shared vocabulary for the L1 Shadow problem.

Strongest site-naming alignment

04 · Fault lines. Direct reference to Finding Fault Lines within the Firm — the source of the site's hero image.

Hybrid sketch

Geological strata below + emerging road on top. Two-axis: depth-of-pressure story plus surface-trail-becoming-network story.
