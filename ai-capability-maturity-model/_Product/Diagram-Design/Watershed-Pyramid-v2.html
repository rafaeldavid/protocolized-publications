The Maturity Pyramid — an inverted watershed.

A continuous waterway that rises against gravity. From scattered springs at the base, through human-cut channels, held by a dam, harnessed by a generator, and finally released as vapor into a planetary ocean. The pyramid is overlaid lightly — it tells you how to read the picture, not what the picture is.

How to read

Read upward: each stage requires the one below it. Without springs there is no channel; without a dam, no generator; without a generator, no vapor.

The pyramid is the reading lens, not the picture. Look through it to know which stage you are on; look past it to see the metaphor itself.

Why a watershed

A pyramid alone implies climbing. A watershed implies flow — a moving substance you are managing, not stones you are stacking.

Inverting the watershed lets the pyramid keep its mnemonic shape while the metaphor underneath says: maturity is the lift against gravity.

Where the metaphor cuts

A real watershed flows down. This one rises — and this is intentional: maturity is not gravity-fed.

The apex is precisely where the work stops mattering, because the system has joined a larger one. The dam dissolves into weather.
