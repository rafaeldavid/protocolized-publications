# Assessment v0.2 — binary forced-choice draft

This draft expands the current 2-step quiz into a **15-question binary forced-choice** flow modeled on `quadrantology.com/test.html`. **Not implemented.** Captured here for reference while we go back and build a proper framework of dimensions grounded in the litepaper bibliography.

## Format

- 15 single questions, one per screen
- Prompt on each: "Choose the statement that resonates more with you:"
- Two statement cards below, user picks one
- Auto-advance after pick with a brief highlight pause; Back button preserves prior picks
- Randomized per session: question order shuffled, A/B left-right position flipped 50/50
- Progress indicator "4 of 15"
- After Q15: compute score → map to placement → render existing result card via `renderOutput(placement)` (unchanged)

## Scoring

Each "higher maturity" pick = 1 pt. Total 0–15.

| Score | Placement | Result card key |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | L1 Shadow | `'1'` |
| 4–6 | L2 Sanctioned | `'2'` |
| 7–9 | L2–3 transition | `'2-3'` |
| 10–12 | L3 Designed | `'3'` |
| 13–15 | L3–4 transition | `'3-4'` |

## Questions (by dimension in this draft)

`[A↑]` / `[B↑]` marks which statement scores 1 pt. Mixed so pattern-picking doesn't work.

### D1 · Visibility & policy
1. **Visibility of AI tool usage** `[B↑]` — A: "We don't track which AI tools our teams use or what data they submit." / B: "AI tool usage is tracked per team with a formal inventory and auditable access."
2. **Data-boundary policy** `[A↑]` — A: "A documented, technically enforced data-boundary policy specifies what can leave the organization." / B: "Employees use their own judgment about what data they share with AI tools."
3. **Personal AI accounts** `[B↑]` (from Samsung) — A: "Employees use personal AI accounts for work tasks without organizational review." / B: "Personal AI accounts are blocked on corporate networks; only sanctioned tools are available."

### D2 · Workflow design
4. **Workflow design posture** `[A↑]` — A: "At least one core workflow is deliberately designed around AI, with a named owner." / B: "AI use is individual; each employee chooses their own tools and methods."
5. **Ownership accountability** `[B↑]` — A: "AI output quality is diffuse; no single person is accountable when something fails." / B: "A named owner is accountable for AI output quality in at least one workflow."
6. **Workflow dependence** `[B↑]` (from Boom) — A: "If our AI tool stopped working, we could pause and revert to the old process." / B: "If our AI tool stopped working, we'd need to rebuild the workflow from scratch."

### D3 · Governance posture
7. **Adoption posture** `[A↑]` — A: "AI adoption is mandated with per-workflow protocols governing how outputs enter delivery." / B: "AI adoption is optional; employees choose if and how to use it."
8. **External quality exposure** `[B↑]` (from Klarna) — A: "We learn about AI-related quality issues after customers complain or incidents surface." / B: "Our protocols catch AI quality issues internally before they reach customers."
9. **Written review protocol** `[A↑]` — A: "A written, auditable protocol for AI output review exists in at least one function." / B: "AI output review varies by team and relies on individual judgment."

### D4 · Incident & response (from existing Step 2 scenarios)
10. **Client-facing AI error** `[B↑]` (from Step 2A) — A: "When an AI error reaches a client, response depends on who notices and how." / B: "When an AI error reaches a client, a defined protocol triggers and we log a response."
11. **Model update handling** `[A↑]` (from Step 2C) — A: "When a vendor updates our AI tool, a review process validates outputs before adoption." / B: "When a vendor updates our AI tool, we keep using it and notice changes over time."
12. **Governance status honesty** `[B↑]` (from Step 2B) — A: "If asked for an honest AI governance status, we'd say enforcement is inconsistent." / B: "Multiple AI-enabled workflows are designed, owned, and tracked with quality metrics."

### D5 · Organizational integration
13. **Executive ownership** `[A↑]` — A: "AI has a named C-level owner accountable for strategy, deployment, and governance." / B: "AI responsibility is distributed across leaders without one accountable owner."
14. **Organizational change** `[B↑]` — A: "Our team structure and hiring haven't changed substantially in response to AI." / B: "We've restructured teams or hiring around AI capability; it's core to our org design."
15. **Value measurement** `[B↑]` — A: "AI's value in our organization is anecdotal; we haven't measured it per workflow." / B: "AI's contribution is measured per workflow with attribution and a clear removal cost."

## Open concerns (2026-04-24 review)

- **Q15 is miscalibrated.** AI-native orgs don't measure AI-ROI the way this implies; "per-workflow attribution with removal cost" isn't a hallmark of Designed+ maturity. Reframe when we rebuild.
- **Dimensions aren't grounded in the litepaper's unique vocabulary.** The 5 dimensions above (visibility / workflow / governance / incident / integration) are generic maturity-model categories. The litepaper's distinctive moves — protocol-centric framing, ontological drift (Benny), orientation (Kreutler), temporal mismatches, bottleneck thinking, non-events (Weick) — don't show up in the draft.
- **Statements are close to plain language but not quite.** Target: layman-friendly register. E.g., replace "technically enforced" with "blocked by our systems" if that reads more natural.
- **Source-material coverage is thin.** We pulled from Samsung / Klarna / Boom and the existing Step 2 scenarios, but the bibliography has ~40 sources worth of conceptual signal that hasn't informed the questions yet.
