# Assessment v0.3 — 15 binary-choice statements

Statements mapped to the 6-dimension framework (see `Framework-v1.md` v1.1). Each question presents two contrasting statements; user picks the one that resonates more. Each statement ≤ 20 words. Layman-friendly voice, no em-dashes, no "X, not Y" marketing structure.

## Question allocation across dimensions

15 questions total. D1 / D3 / D4 get 3 each (highest-signal dimensions for an operational-leader diagnostic in the L1–L3 range). D2 / D5 / D6 get 2 each (important but more narrowly load-bearing).

| Dimension | Questions | Why this weighting |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Protocol specificity | Q1–Q3 (3) | Core thesis of the model; worth fine-grained distinction between "no protocol", "policy-style protocol", "handoff-specified protocol" |
| D2 Ontological stability | Q4–Q5 (2) | Important at L3/4 but less visible to most respondents; 2 is enough to catch the drift vs. stability signal |
| D3 Temporal alignment | Q6–Q8 (3) | Canonical L2→L3 failure mode (review can't keep up); also capacity planning + stakeholder-rhythm signals |
| D4 Organizational visibility | Q9–Q11 (3) | Canonical L1→L2 failure mode; needs three because the invisibility paradox requires both directions represented |
| D5 Domain expertise leverage | Q12–Q13 (2) | 2 is enough to catch generic-vs-specialized and floor-vs-ceiling signals |
| D6 Coordination scope | Q14–Q15 (2) | Scope mostly matters at L3/4; 2 questions surface internal-vs-external reach and kitification |

## Scoring

Each "higher" pick = 1 pt. Total 0–15.

| Score | Placement | Result card key |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | L1 Shadow | `'1'` |
| 4–6 | L2 Sanctioned | `'2'` |
| 7–9 | L2–3 transition | `'2-3'` |
| 10–12 | L3 Designed | `'3'` |
| 13–15 | L3–4 transition | `'3-4'` |

Simple sum-based placement in v0.3. Bottleneck logic (minimum-across-dimensions) is deferred to a later iteration once we have real-world data to validate the dimension weighting.

## The 15 statements

`[A↑]` / `[B↑]` indicates which statement is the "higher maturity" side (scores 1 pt). Presentation order and A/B left-right position are randomized per session at runtime; this document uses a canonical order for review.

### D1 Protocol specificity (3 questions)

**Q1. Rules at AI handoff points.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* smooth-to-striated, protocol vs. policy
- A: "We have specific rules for when AI output needs human review before going out."
- B: "We have general guidance on AI use but no specific rules about when to review output."

**Q2. What our AI guidelines cover.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* protocol vs. policy distinction, handoff contracts
- A: "Our AI guidelines say what's allowed but don't specify what happens at handoffs."
- B: "Our AI guidelines spell out what happens at each handoff between AI and human work."

**Q3. Practitioner understanding of the protocol.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* human-protocol alignment (Rao/Nast bus-bunching), practitioner legibility
- A: "The people running our AI processes can explain why the protocol works, not just follow it."
- B: "Our people follow AI guidelines as rules without necessarily knowing why they matter."

### D2 Ontological stability (2 questions)

**Q4. Stability of key terms and schemas.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* ontological drift (Benny), schema freezing
- A: "Key terms and data definitions shift often enough that AI tools drift quickly from reality."
- B: "Our key terms and schemas stay stable long enough for AI systems to rely on them."

**Q5. Foundational craft preservation.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* layers of fixity, Roman-concrete risk (Benny)
- A: "We keep some people working on lower-layer craft so AI-assisted work has a foundation."
- B: "Most of our team works AI-assisted; foundational skills are drifting to external vendors."

### D3 Temporal alignment (3 questions)

**Q6. Review pace vs. generation pace.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* Slacker Index, AI-speed vs. review-speed mismatch, Uber code-review bottleneck
- A: "AI generates work faster than our review and audit processes can keep up with."
- B: "Our review and audit processes keep up with the pace AI is generating work."

**Q7. Capacity planning under uneven AI change.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* Task Estimation Crisis (Stinson-Schroff)
- A: "We've adjusted how we plan capacity now that AI trivializes some tasks and not others."
- B: "Our capacity planning has struggled since AI started trivializing some tasks but not others."

**Q8. Stakeholder rhythm mismatch.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* sync vs. async stakeholders (Benny, *The Two Memories*)
- A: "Some roles want AI always-on; others prefer batch. The mismatch creates friction."
- B: "Roles that want always-on AI and roles that want batch coexist without conflict."

### D4 Organizational visibility (3 questions)

**Q9. Tool-use visibility.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* perception precedes policy (blog post), shadow AI detection
- A: "We can list which AI tools our employees use and roughly what data flows through them."
- B: "We don't really know which AI tools our employees use day-to-day or what data they share."

**Q10. Shadow AI detection mechanism.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* Samsung case, Litepaper L1 blind spot
- A: "Shadow AI use probably happens here, but we don't have ways to detect it."
- B: "We have ways to spot when employees use AI tools we haven't sanctioned."

**Q11. Successful invisibility of mature protocols.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* progress through invisibility, Whitehead advance (Rao/Nast, *Theorizing Protocolization I*)
- A: "Our core AI protocols run reliably in the background without taking management attention."
- B: "Our AI protocols need constant management attention to stay on the rails."

**Note on Q11.** This is the "invisibility paradox" question. Scoring A↑ means mature orgs (whose protocols have faded into reliable background) score this correctly, as do poorly-designed L1/L2 orgs with no protocols at all. Q9 and Q10 separate L1/L2 from L3+ based on whether visibility is *achievable* (orgs that can see tool use vs. can't). Combined, the three D4 questions distinguish: L1 (invisible & untrackable), L2 (visible-ish with effort), L3+ (designed for reliable fade).

### D5 Domain expertise leverage (2 questions)

**Q12. Who validates AI output.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* domain expertise as higher-level perception (Litepaper L3), FDE pattern
- A: "People with deep domain expertise validate AI output in our most critical workflows."
- B: "Generic compliance or checklists validate AI output, without deep domain context."

**Q13. Floor-raising vs. ceiling-raising.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* Time to Mediocrity (Stinson-Schroff), bricoleur framing
- A: "AI mostly raises the floor, letting less-expert people do passable work across domains."
- B: "AI helps our experts go further in their specialty, not replacing expertise."

### D6 Coordination scope (2 questions)

**Q14. Internal vs. external reach.** `[A↑]`
*Anchors:* agent handoff contracts, EDI historical parallel, Bristlemouth sectoral design
- A: "Our AI protocols extend beyond our walls to partners, vendors, or industry standards."
- B: "Our AI protocols only govern what happens inside our own organization."

**Q15. Reusable capability infrastructure.** `[B↑]`
*Anchors:* kitification, Brain vs. Fabric
- A: "Our AI capabilities are bespoke; they don't easily extend to new teams or partners."
- B: "Our AI capabilities are built as reusable kits that could scale to new teams or partners."

## Open questions for review

1. **Q7 phrasing on "trivializes."** The word "trivializes" captures the Task Estimation Crisis phenomenon precisely but might feel jargon-y. Alternative: "makes much easier" or "largely automates."
2. **Q11 invisibility paradox.** This is the riskiest question — both the "bad" L1 state (no protocols, no management attention needed) and the "good" L5 state (reliable background) could resonate with A. The question assumes the respondent is thinking about protocols they have, not ones they lack. Worth stress-testing with a sample reader.
3. **Q13 floor vs. ceiling.** Both can be true simultaneously in a big org — some teams floor-raising, others ceiling-raising. The statement forces a pick of which is *more* true. OK or should we ask about the bottleneck function specifically?
4. **Q15 kitification framing.** "Kits" is your coinage; might not be instantly readable to an outside ops leader. Alternatives: "reusable templates", "standardized modules".
