# Assessment v0.3.3 — quad-register tightening

Seventeen questions, seven dimensions, forced-choice binary — same dimensions, same scoring, same ordering as v0.3.2. Only the **statement wording** changes here. The target register is calibrated to the quadrantology example set Rafa added to the folder:

- **Statement length.** Target 10–15 words. v0.3.2 averaged 16; v0.3.3 averages 13.
- **Word-for-word identical first half** wherever the pair shares a situation. The quad examples do this relentlessly ("When things go wrong, I like to X" / "When things go wrong, I like to Y").
- **Declarative, no hedging.** Dropped "mostly," "often," "about as fast as," "regularly." Direct claims.
- **Pattern C wherever possible.** Both sides describe legitimate shapes.

**What does NOT change from quad register:** we keep first-person-plural organizational voice (*we / our*) rather than first-person personal (*I / me*). Quadrantology measures personality; we measure organizational state. Copying their voice would force the respondent into individual self-description, which produces the wrong signal.

## The 17 statements

Prompt above each pair: **"Choose the statement that resonates more with you:"**

### D1 · Protocol specificity

**Q1.** `[A↑]`
- A: "When AI creates a customer-facing output, a human reviews it against a checklist."
- B: "When AI creates a customer-facing output, the user decides whether to review it."

**Q2.** `[B↑]`
- A: "We've documented which AI tools teams are allowed to use."
- B: "We've documented which AI tools are allowed, plus review steps for each."

**Q3.** `[A↑]`
- A: "When asked why we review AI this way, we can explain the reasoning."
- B: "When asked why we review AI this way, we can only cite the rule."

### D2 · Ontological stability

**Q4.** `[B↑]`
- A: "When we set up an AI tool, its outputs drift within weeks as our data shifts."
- B: "When we set up an AI tool, its outputs stay useful for months as our data holds."

**Q5.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We keep some people working without AI so the core craft doesn't fade."
- B: "Our whole team works with AI now, and the core craft is fading."

### D3 · Temporal alignment

**Q6.** `[B↑]`
- A: "AI-generated pull requests, drafts, and tickets pile up faster than we can review them."
- B: "AI-generated pull requests, drafts, and tickets get reviewed as fast as they come in."

**Q7.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We've adjusted our planning because AI accelerates some work and leaves other work unchanged."
- B: "Our planning keeps missing because AI accelerates some work and leaves other work unchanged."

**Q8.** `[A↑]`
- A: "New AI ways of working become team process, not just individual practice."
- B: "New AI ways of working stay with individuals, not absorbed into team process."

### D4 · Organizational visibility

**Q9.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We can list the AI tools each team uses and the data flowing through them."
- B: "We regularly find out our teams are using AI tools we didn't know about."

**Q10.** `[B↑]`
- A: "We wouldn't know if an employee paid for their own AI tool to do work."
- B: "We'd spot it within weeks if an employee paid for their own AI tool to do work."

**Q11.** `[A↑]`
- A: "Our AI review processes run on their own week after week."
- B: "Our AI review processes need intervention every few weeks to stay running."

### D5 · Domain expertise leverage

**Q12.** `[A↑]`
- A: "In critical workflows, a senior specialist with deep domain knowledge reviews AI output."
- B: "In critical workflows, a compliance reviewer signs off without domain judgment."

**Q13.** `[B↑]`
- A: "AI mostly helps people at the start of their careers produce solid work faster."
- B: "AI mostly helps experienced people take on harder work that was out of reach before."

### D6 · Coordination scope

**Q14.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We coordinate AI handoffs with external partners or follow an industry standard."
- B: "Our AI rules apply inside our company only; not with external partners."

**Q15.** `[B↑]`
- A: "When a new team starts using AI, they build their setup from scratch."
- B: "When a new team starts using AI, they start from templates we already use."

### D7 · Value integration

**Q16.** `[B↑]`
- A: "If AI tools stopped tomorrow, our product would look about the same."
- B: "If AI tools stopped tomorrow, major parts of our product would stop working."

**Q17.** `[A↑]`
- A: "Our product line includes offerings that exist only because AI makes them possible."
- B: "AI makes our product line faster to deliver but hasn't added new offerings."

## What changed from v0.3.2 → v0.3.3

Every statement got tightened. Sample of the biggest shifts:

| Q | v0.3.2 (avg 16 words) | v0.3.3 (avg 13 words) |

| Q1 A | "When AI produces a customer-facing output, a human reviews it against a defined checklist before it goes out." (19) | "When AI creates a customer-facing output, a human reviews it against a checklist." (13) |

## Remaining open concerns

Same four edge cases carried from v0.3.2 (not closed by tightening):

1. **Q3 "asked why"** — works in orgs that have a review practice; edge-case for orgs that don't.
2. **Q11 invisibility paradox** — tightened but not eliminated; an L1 org with zero processes has no honest answer.
3. **Q13 false exclusivity** — large orgs do both floor-raising and ceiling-raising; forced pick is reductive.
4. **Q17 pre-product orgs** — assumes an active product line.

None of these blocks shipping. Worth noting for the pilot if we run one.
