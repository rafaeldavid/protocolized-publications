# Assessment archetypes — v0.3

v0.2 had a structural problem: **"Over-Reliant"** as an archetype name fails the self-identification test. No respondent picks an option labeled as a pathology. A diagnostic that routes readers by self-selection has to offer legitimate strategies on every side — including the one we previously framed as an anti-pattern.

This version applies the **positivity principle** to every archetype: each one reads as a defensible strategy with a coherent upside. The trade-offs and risks live in the descriptor copy, not the name.

## The positivity principle (v0.3 addition)

> Every archetype must read as a legitimate organizational strategy a respondent would willingly identify with. Trade-offs and known failure modes go in the descriptor, not in the archetype label.

This doesn't mean we hide the risks. It means we frame the risk as **the cost of the strategy**, not as a personal failure. "I accept higher reversal risk in exchange for going first" is a recognizable trade; "I am Over-Reliant" is an accusation.

Applied to the v0.2 set:

| v0.2 name | v0.3 name | Reframe logic |
|---|---|---|
| **The Over-Reliant** (anti-pattern) | **The Pioneer** | The strength is willingness to bet early. Pioneers get shot first and plant flags first. The descriptor owns the reversal risk as the cost of being first. |
| **The Platform Builder** | **The Platform Builder** (kept — sharpened) | Already positive. Descriptor now emphasizes institutional evolution: patient capital, durable infrastructure, long-horizon transformation. |
| **The AI-Native Builder** | **The AI-Native Builder** (kept — broadened) | Two legitimate flavors now explicit: market-creating (Boom, Cursor) and service-rebuilding (AI-native fintech / logistics competing in existing markets with leaner operating models). |
| **The Deferrer** | **The Deliberate Adopter** | Apple pattern: adopt later, deploy with high polish. The strength is avoiding volatility and deploying mature tech at higher impact. |

## The three anchor archetypes (v0.3)

### Anchor 1 · **The Pragmatist** (L2–3)

**Strategy.** Adopt AI selectively, where value is demonstrable. Redesign workflows around AI rather than layering AI on existing processes. Measured, evidence-driven.

**Strength in their own words.** "We're not first, but we're not lost. We adopt where it pays, and we redesign when it matters."

**Cost they accept.** Slower than first-movers; will miss some compounding advantages that went to organizations willing to take earlier risk.

**Canonical example.** Accenture "AI Achievers" (12% of enterprises); McKinsey high performers who are 3× more likely to fundamentally redesign workflows.

**Dimension signature.** Mid-to-high D1, high D2, mid D3, high D5, mid D7, mid D6. AI-flavor emphasis: 2 + 3.

**Maturity.** L2–3.

---

### Anchor 2 · **The Pioneer** (L2, high-risk high-reward)

**Strategy.** Deploy AI aggressively in core or customer-facing work, often before governance is ready. Accept that some bets will reverse. Plant flags. Learn in public.

**Strength in their own words.** "Someone has to go first. We'd rather learn hard than wait safe."

**Cost they accept.** Reversal risk — public mandate rollbacks, customer-facing quality incidents, brand impact. Pioneers plant flags; they also get shot.

**Canonical example.** Klarna (2024–2025 AI-agent arc: mandated, reversed, rebuilt); Duolingo (AI-first content, user DAU headwinds); Shopify (Lütke "non-optional" memo, strong revenues, reversal risk not yet materialized).

**Dimension signature.** Very high D7, high adoption-rate across flavors 2+3, low D1 relative to D7 (governance lags ambition), low D5 (generic validation rather than domain-expert).

**Maturity.** L2 with known reversal risk. The diagnostic value is specifically in naming this shape — it looks advanced on D7 but is structurally exposed.

**Design note.** The descriptor should own the trade-off. Something like: "We've moved fast on AI in our core work. Some of it will need to be rolled back — that's the cost of going first."

---

### Anchor 3 · **The Platform Builder** (L3–4)

**Strategy.** Invest in internal AI infrastructure that multiple teams depend on. Treat validation and governance as engineered constraints built into the platform. Institutional-scale evolution rather than tactical rollout.

**Strength in their own words.** "We're not chasing the latest model. We're building the platform our teams will run on for years."

**Cost they accept.** Slower to market than startups; capital-intensive; internal coordination overhead.

**Canonical example.** Uber (four-layer platform architecture, 84% developer agentic adoption, code review scaled alongside generation, 6× cost increase since 2024). BBVA (4000+ custom GPTs). Frontier orgs in OpenAI's State of Enterprise AI (6× messages vs. median).

**Dimension signature.** High D1 + D2 + D3 + D5, mid-high D6, mid-high D7. AI-flavor emphasis: 4 + 5 central; the platform provides 2 + 3 to other teams.

**Maturity.** L3, moving toward L4.

**Extra signal from vibe-coding pass.** Platform Builder's competitive moat strengthens with every rapid iterator's failed prototype — the infrastructure layer absorbs upstream signal. Worth noting in the descriptor as a structural advantage.

## Extensions (6)

### Ext 1 · **The AI-Native Builder** (L3)

Product architecture depends on AI from inception. **Two legitimate flavors:**
- **Market-creating** — AI enables new capabilities or offerings that weren't possible before (Boom Supersonic, Cursor).
- **Service-rebuilding** — AI-native operating model competing in existing markets with 3–5× leaner structure (AI-native fintechs, logistics, customer service).

Strength: no legacy baggage. Every protocol is fresh. Captured 44% of 2025 AI VC investment; moves 3.6× faster than incumbents.

### Ext 2 · **The Deliberate Adopter** (L1–2)

**Apple pattern.** Adopts late but with high polish. Waits for vendor consolidation, regulatory clarity, and proven use cases. When they move, they deploy at scale with refinement the early-movers skipped.

**Strength in their own words.** "We don't surf the first wave. We ship the one that lasts."

**Cost they accept.** Missed windows of differentiation that went to the Pragmatists and Pioneers who moved earlier.

Real-world example: Apple's historical pattern with emerging technologies (consumer software categories, AR, and arguably AI itself). Risk-averse leaders navigating governance confusion often fall in this archetype deliberately.

### Ext 3 · **The Compliant-First** (L2)

Regulation-driven adoption. Gates deployment until EU AI Act or vertical-specific compliance is confirmed. Compliance as competitive moat (1.5–2× valuation premium for compliance-ready assets in regulated verticals). EU orgs, financial services, healthcare.

### Ext 4 · **The Rapid Iterator** (L1–2) — formerly FAFO Experimenter

Scrappy, disciplined experimentation. Runs many pilots; codifies what works into hardened processes. Less risk-averse than Pragmatist, less mandated than Pioneer. Most common in scaleups.

**Name note.** "FAFO Experimenter" preserved the user's reference but wasn't viable for reader-facing copy. "Rapid Iterator" keeps the substance — speed + learning — without the irreverent surface.

### Ext 5 · **The Invisible Experts** (L2–3)

Individual domain experts doing sophisticated AI work below organizational visibility. F2F pattern from Rao's *Have Your Factory Call My Factory*. Valuable if codified; Samsung-style risk if truly invisible.

**Sharpened via the vibe-coding pass.** These experts are accumulating what *Vibe Coding and the Maker Movement* calls "tasteful discrimination" — pattern recognition about what actually works that the base models don't possess. They're building proprietary signal value that the organization may not realize it has. The diagnostic value of this archetype is catching the asymmetry between where the expertise lives (individual) and where the org thinks it lives (tools, policies).

### Ext 6 · **The Organic Adopter** (L1) — formerly Accidental Adopter

Bottom-up adoption. AI arrived through team initiative before a top-down strategy formed. The org has a real phenomenon to reckon with; the question is whether they catch up to their own practitioners.

**Name note.** "Accidental" framed the state as a mistake. "Organic" frames it as an emergent strength — your teams are already discovering valuable use cases. The diagnostic signal is the same (org strategy hasn't caught up to individual adoption); the framing is honest about what's valuable.

## Integration: vibe coding + maker movement pass

[*Vibe Coding and the Maker Movement*](https://read.technically.dev/p/vibe-coding-and-the-maker-movement) argues that vibe coding skipped the traditional "scenius" phase where hobbyists develop judgment through extended play, deploying directly to production. This creates evaluative dysfunction — producers can't distinguish valuable output from productivity theater — while value accumulates upstream at the infrastructure layer rather than with the makers themselves.

Key terms worth keeping in our vocabulary:

- **"Crapjects"** — outputs that prove capability exists but serve no purpose. Adjacent to Pioneer's risk: moving fast without evaluation capacity.
- **"Evaluative anesthesia"** — inability to judge output quality when production is fast and cheap. Strengthens the Pragmatist vs. Pioneer distinction (Pragmatist preserves evaluation; Pioneer may not).
- **"Signal capture before upstream absorption"** — Platform Builders benefit from iterative exhaust upstream. Reinforces Platform Builder's moat.

**Did the article surface a missing archetype?** The researcher suggested "The Consumption Performer" (AI deployment as attention-generation rather than product-building). I'm skeptical — it overlaps with Pioneer (both deploy visibly; both accept reversal risk) without offering orthogonal diagnostic value. Skipping for now; can revisit if reader data shows a distinct cluster.

**Did it sharpen existing archetypes?** Yes, three:
1. **Invisible Experts** — now explicit about "tasteful discrimination" as proprietary signal value
2. **Platform Builder** — now explicit about upstream value capture as structural moat
3. **Pragmatist vs. Pioneer distinction** — evaluative anesthesia is a key behavioral difference

## Recommended quiz format (unchanged from v0.2)

**Model B′ — Screen 1 strategy pick + Screen 2 behavioral validator.**

Screen 1 presents the 3 anchors (+ maybe 1–2 extensions that matter most for the target audience). Reader picks one. Screen 2 asks about concrete recent behavior to validate or downgrade. Combined result → archetype + maturity level.

**Which extensions to surface on Screen 1?** Open design question. Options:
- **3 anchors only** (Pragmatist / Pioneer / Platform Builder) — cleanest, forces Screen 2 to discriminate sub-patterns
- **3 anchors + Deliberate Adopter + Organic Adopter** — covers the L1 pattern explicitly
- **All 9 archetypes** — too many for one screen; loses the anchor-first clarity

My vote: **3 anchors + 2 extensions = 5 options**, pairing Anchors with Deliberate Adopter and Organic Adopter. Those two catch the L1 patterns that otherwise collapse into "none of the above."

## Open questions

1. **Do the reframes land?** "The Pioneer" for the Klarna pattern is the big bet — it has to be honest about reversal risk while being self-selectable. Descriptor copy will make or break this.
2. **"Deliberate Adopter" name.** Alternatives: "The Late Refiner", "The Polished Adopter", "The Strategic Late-Mover". My pick is "Deliberate" because it emphasizes intention, which is what separates this archetype from Deferrer-as-laziness.
3. **"Rapid Iterator" name.** Alternatives: "The Experimenter", "The Trial-and-Learner", "The Discovery-Driven". My pick is "Rapid Iterator" because it captures both speed and the iteration discipline.
4. **Screen 1 option count.** 3 anchors, 5 anchors+extensions, or all 9?
