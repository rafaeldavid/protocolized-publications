# Assessment v0.3.2 — red-team-informed rewrite

Seventeen questions, seven dimensions, forced-choice binary. Every question has been rewritten against the three remediation patterns from `Red-Team-v0.3.1.md`:

- **Pattern A** — parallel construction around a shared situation ("When X, we Y" / "When X, we Z")
- **Pattern B** — both sides affirm, one goes further (kills the "obvious good answer" bias)
- **Pattern C** — both sides describe legitimate organizational shapes, not good/bad

Additional fixes applied:
- Q8 replaced (sync/batch concept dropped, intensification-absorption framing from Ranganathan/Ye substituted)
- Typos fixed, incomplete statements completed
- Escape-hatch language removed ("or has the potential to" etc.)
- Q11 tightened to avoid the invisibility-paradox edge case
- All statements ≤ 20 words, no em-dashes

## Scoring (unchanged from v0.3.1)

Each "higher-maturity" pick = 1 pt. Total 0–17.

## The 17 statements

`[A↑]` / `[B↑]` indicates the higher-maturity side. Order and left/right position randomized at runtime.

### D1 · Protocol specificity

**Q1. Review at AI handoff points.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "When AI produces a customer-facing output, a human reviews it against a defined checklist before it goes out."
- B: "When AI produces a customer-facing output, the person using the tool decides case by case whether to review."

**Q2. Scope of the AI rules.** `[B↑]` — Pattern B (both affirm, B goes further)
- A: "We've documented which AI tools our teams are allowed to use."
- B: "We've documented which AI tools are allowed, plus the specific review steps inside each allowed use."

**Q3. Why, not just what.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "When a new hire asks why we review AI output this way, someone can explain the reasoning."
- B: "When a new hire asks why we review AI output this way, most people can only cite the rule."

### D2 · Ontological stability

**Q4. Definitions under AI tools.** `[B↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "When we set up a new AI tool, its outputs drift within weeks because our data shifts underneath it."
- B: "When we set up a new AI tool, its outputs stay useful for months because our data definitions hold stable."

**Q5. Foundational craft preservation.** `[A↑]` — Pattern C (both are real strategies)
- A: "We deliberately keep some people on core craft without AI, so we don't lose the underlying skill."
- B: "Our team has moved to AI-assisted work across the board, and the core craft is fading."

### D3 · Temporal alignment

**Q6. Review pace vs. generation pace.** `[B↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "AI-generated pull requests, drafts, or tickets pile up faster than our reviewers can process them."
- B: "AI-generated pull requests, drafts, or tickets get reviewed about as fast as they come in."

**Q7. Capacity planning under uneven AI change.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "We've updated our planning to account for AI speeding up some work and leaving other work unchanged."
- B: "Our planning keeps missing because AI speeds up some work and leaves other work unchanged."

**Q8. Intensification vs. absorption.** `[A↑]` — NEW (replaces sync/batch) · Pattern C
- A: "New AI-driven ways of working get absorbed into team process, not stuck with individual practitioners."
- B: "Our people are working harder with AI, but the new ways of working stay with individuals."

### D4 · Organizational visibility

**Q9. Tool-use inventory.** `[A↑]` — polished from v0.3.1
- A: "We can list the AI tools each team uses and the data that flows through them."
- B: "We're regularly surprised by which AI tools our teams are actually using."

**Q10. Catching unsanctioned use.** `[B↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "If an employee paid for their own AI tool and used it for work tasks, we wouldn't know."
- B: "If an employee paid for their own AI tool and used it for work tasks, we'd spot it within weeks."

**Q11. Protocols that run themselves.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A, invisibility-paradox edge case fixed
- A: "Our AI review processes run week after week on their own, without needing to be reset."
- B: "Our AI review processes need to be reset or intervened with every few weeks."

### D5 · Domain expertise leverage

**Q12. Who signs off on AI output.** `[A↑]` — minor polish (removed "cold" idiom)
- A: "In critical workflows, a senior specialist with deep domain knowledge reviews AI output."
- B: "In critical workflows, a compliance reviewer or checklist signs off without deep domain judgment."

**Q13. Floor or ceiling.** `[B↑]` — Pattern C (both are legitimate shapes)
- A: "AI mostly helps people at the start of their expertise curve produce solid work faster."
- B: "AI mostly helps people deep in their expertise take on harder work that used to be out of reach."

### D6 · Coordination scope

**Q14. Reach beyond our walls.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A, specificity-balanced
- A: "We coordinate AI handoffs with at least one external partner or follow a recognized industry standard."
- B: "Our AI rules apply inside our company only; we don't coordinate AI handoffs with partners."

**Q15. Reusable vs. bespoke.** `[B↑]` — Pattern A
- A: "When a new team starts using AI, they mostly build their setup from scratch."
- B: "When a new team starts using AI, they start from templates or configuration guides we already use."

### D7 · Value integration

**Q16. AI absence test.** `[B↑]` — Pattern A, simplified
- A: "If AI tools stopped working tomorrow, our product and service offerings would look about the same."
- B: "If AI tools stopped working tomorrow, major parts of our product would stop working."

**Q17. AI in the product shape.** `[A↑]` — Pattern A, escape-hatch removed
- A: "Our product line includes offerings that exist only because AI makes them possible."
- B: "AI makes our existing product line faster or cheaper to deliver, but hasn't added new offerings."

## Voice-check summary

The register landed a little more precise and a little less idiomatic than v0.3.1 on purpose — the red-team found most of v0.3.1's idioms were carrying desirability bias ("knows the domain cold", "feels in sync", "might have the potential to"). The v0.3.2 voice:

- More "when X, we Y" structure (Pattern A is load-bearing)
- More specific artifact mentions (pull requests, drafts, tickets, tools)
- Less evaluative language ("the right way", "notable increase", "at scale")
- Parallel grammar on both sides of every pair

## Remaining concerns to flag for pilot testing

Even after remediation, four items deserve reader-stress-testing before ship:

1. **Q3 "new hire"** — assumes recent hiring. Most orgs have this; small/static teams won't. Fallback: re-word as "When someone unfamiliar with the setup asks…" if needed.
2. **Q11 invisibility paradox** — better than v0.3.1 but not perfect. An L1 org with no AI review processes will struggle to pick either side honestly. Accept the noise; D4 Q9 + Q10 should catch L1 via visibility scoring.
3. **Q13 false exclusivity** — large orgs are doing both (some teams floor-raising, others ceiling-raising). The forced-pick asks respondents to choose which is *more* dominant; may still feel reductive.
4. **Q17 "AI-only offerings"** — early-stage enterprises may answer "neither" if they're pre-product. Assume target audience (operational leaders in active enterprises) has a product to evaluate.
