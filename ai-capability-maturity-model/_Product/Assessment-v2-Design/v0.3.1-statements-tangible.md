# Assessment v0.3.1 — tangible-voice rewrite

Same 15 questions, same dimension allocation, same scoring bands as v0.3. Every statement is rewritten to be more **tangible**:

- Specific artifacts (pull requests, drafts, tickets, ChatGPT Plus subscriptions, templates) instead of "AI output"
- Specific actors (senior specialists, new hires, compliance reviewers, junior staff) instead of "people"
- Specific time signals (within weeks, week after week, for months at a time) instead of "regularly"
- Observable situations ("a new team wants to adopt AI") instead of abstract state descriptions
- Jargon replaced: "protocol" and "handoff" survive only where load-bearing; "trivializes" → "accelerates"; "kits" → "templates"

All statements ≤ 20 words. No em-dashes.

## The 15 statements

`[A↑]` / `[B↑]` = higher-maturity side. Runtime randomizes order and left/right position per session.

### D1 Protocol specificity

**Q1. Rules before AI output goes out.** `[A↑]`
- A: "Before AI-generated text, code, or decisions go out, a human reviews them against a defined checklist."
- B: "We tell people to be careful with AI, but there's no defined checkpoint before output goes out."

**Q2. What the AI rules actually cover.** `[B↑]`
- A: "Our AI rules cover what tools are allowed, but not what to do at review points."
- B: "Our AI rules spell out exactly what happens at each point where AI work hands off to a human."

**Q3. Why, not just what.** `[A↑]`
- A: "When a new hire asks why we review AI drafts this way, someone can explain the reasoning."
- B: "New hires follow our AI review steps, but most can't explain why the steps are set up this way."

### D2 Ontological stability

**Q4. Field names and categories.** `[B↑]`
- A: "We've had to retrain or reprompt AI tools because field names and categories kept changing underneath them."
- B: "Once we set AI tools up with our data, the underlying definitions stay stable for months at a time."

**Q5. Craft without the AI.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We still have people on the team who can do the core craft without AI assistance."
- B: "Our team has become reliant enough on AI that raw craft skills are fading."

### D3 Temporal alignment

**Q6. Review queues.** `[B↑]`
- A: "AI-generated pull requests, drafts, or tickets pile up faster than humans can review them."
- B: "Humans can review AI-generated pull requests, drafts, or tickets about as fast as they come in."

**Q7. Estimates that hold up.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We've changed how we estimate timelines to account for AI accelerating some steps but not others."
- B: "Our project estimates keep missing because AI makes some steps much faster while others are unchanged."

**Q8. Sync vs. batch teams.** `[B↑]`
- A: "One team wants constant AI help; another wants a weekly batch review. We're still arguing about it."
- B: "Teams that use AI continuously and teams that use it in batches work together without friction."

### D4 Organizational visibility

**Q9. Tool-use inventory.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We can list the AI tools each team uses and roughly what data flows through them."
- B: "We can't tell you which AI tools our people use day-to-day or what they upload to them."

**Q10. Catching unsanctioned use.** `[B↑]`
- A: "If an employee paid for their own ChatGPT Plus and used it for work, we wouldn't know."
- B: "If an employee started using an unsanctioned AI tool for work, we'd spot it within weeks."

**Q11. Does it run itself.** `[A↑]`
- A: "Our AI review processes run week after week without needing leadership to chase or reset them."
- B: "Our AI governance needs leadership intervention every few weeks to keep from falling apart."

### D5 Domain expertise leverage

**Q12. Who signs off.** `[A↑]`
- A: "In critical workflows, a senior specialist who knows the domain cold reviews AI output."
- B: "In critical workflows, a compliance reviewer or checklist signs off without deep domain judgment."

**Q13. Floor or ceiling.** `[B↑]`
- A: "AI mostly helps junior staff produce acceptable work in areas where they'd otherwise struggle."
- B: "Our senior specialists use AI to do harder, more creative work that was out of reach before."

### D6 Coordination scope

**Q14. Reach beyond our walls.** `[A↑]`
- A: "We coordinate AI handoffs with at least one external partner or follow a recognized industry standard."
- B: "Our AI rules only apply inside our company; nothing extends to partners or vendors."

**Q15. Reusable vs. bespoke.** `[B↑]`
- A: "When a new team wants to adopt AI, we build something custom for them from scratch."
- B: "When a new team wants to adopt AI, they can start from a template we already use."

## Remaining open concerns

1. **Q3 on "new hire" framing** — the question is about practitioner legibility. Assumes the org has hired recently. Most orgs do. If the respondent is at a very small/static org, the framing still works because "can someone explain the reasoning" is the underlying test.
2. **Q10 ChatGPT Plus example** — very concrete but ties to a specific product. Works in 2026; risk of dating in a year. Alternative: "their own AI subscription."
3. **Q11 invisibility paradox risk** — still a known edge case. An L1 org with no protocols at all might pick "A" (nothing to chase because nothing exists). Mitigation: Q9 + Q10 catch the L1 state via visibility failure; combined scores should resolve correctly. Worth validating when we run real responses.
4. **Q12 "knows the domain cold"** — idiomatic. Might feel American English. Alternatives: "has deep experience" or "deeply familiar with the domain".
