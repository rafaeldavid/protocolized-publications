# Assessment v0.3.1 — tangible-voice rewrite

Same 15 questions, same dimension allocation, same scoring bands as v0.3. Every statement is rewritten to be more **tangible**:

- Specific artifacts (pull requests, drafts, tickets, ChatGPT Plus subscriptions, templates) instead of "AI output"
- Specific actors (senior specialists, new hires, compliance reviewers, junior staff) instead of "people"
- Specific time signals (within weeks, week after week, for months at a time) instead of "regularly"
- Observable situations ("a new team wants to adopt AI") instead of abstract state descriptions
- Jargon replaced: "protocol" and "handoff" survive only where load-bearing; "trivializes" → "accelerates"; "kits" → "templates"

All statements ≤ 20 words. No em-dashes.

## The 15 statements

`[A↑]` / `[B↑]` = higher-maturity side. Runtime randomizes order and left/right position per session.

### D1 Protocol specificity

**Q1. Rules before AI output goes out.** `[A↑]`

- A: "AI-supported text, code, and decisions are mostly high quality."
- B: "We tell people to be careful with AI tools. Their outputs may be wrong."

**Q2. What the AI rules actually cover.** `[B↑]`

- A: "We have formal or informal rules about what AI tooling is allowed."
- B: "We have guidance for the policies, tools, and tests that review AI-enabled outputs."

**Q3. Why, not just what.** `[A↑]`

- A: "Most people can explain how AI supports our business offering."
- B: "When a new hire asks about AI usage, most people can't explain easily."

### D2 Ontological stability

**Q4. Field names and categories.** `[B↑]`

- A: "We've had to constantly adjust AI tool settings because they consistently drift or cause confusion."
- B: "Once we integrate an AI tool or capality, perfomance is reliable for months at a time."

**Q5. Craft without the AI.** `[A↑]`

- A: "Out team could achieve their objectives without AI assistance or software."
- B: "Our team perfomance notably increases when they integrate AI into their workflows."

### D3 Temporal alignment

**Q6. Review queues.** `[B↑]`

- A: "AI-generated outputs are piling up faster than we can process them."
- B: "Our business feels in sync with our AI-supported product development."

**Q7. Estimates that hold up.** `[A↑]`

- A: "We can plan well regardless of AI usage and product capabilities."
- B: "It's becoming more difficult to estimate timelines, especially when teams adopt new AI tools."

**Q8. Sync vs. batch teams.** `[B↑]`

- A: "One team wants constant AI help; another wants a weekly batch review. We're still arguing about it."
- B: "Team members that use AI continuously work together well with teams that ."

### D4 Organizational visibility

**Q9. Tool-use inventory.** `[A↑]`

- A: "We can list the AI tools each team uses and roughly what data flows through them."
- B: "We're often surprised by what AI tools are used, and how they are used."

**Q10. Catching unsanctioned use.** `[B↑]`

- A: "We wouldn't know if an employee paid for their own AI tool and used it for work."
- B: "It's unlikely an employee would use unsafe AI tools."

**Q11. Does it run itself.** `[A↑]`

- A: "Our quality control processes run week after week without needing intervention."
- B: "We're constantly fixing issues related to AI usage."

### D5 Domain expertise leverage

**Q12. Who signs off.** `[A↑]`

- A: "In critical AI-enabled workflows, a senior specialist who knows the domain audits the output."
- B: "In critical AI-enabled workflows, a compliance reviewer or checklist signs off without deep domain judgment."

**Q13. Floor or ceiling.** `[B↑]`

- A: "AI mostly helps junior staff produce acceptable work in areas where they'd otherwise struggle."
- B: "Experienced team members use AI to do harder, more creative work at scale that was out of reach before."

### D6 Coordination scope

**Q14. Reach beyond our walls.** `[A↑]`

- A: "Vendors and clients are working with us to develop new AI-usage standards."
- B: "Our partners are not impacted by changes in our AI capabilities."

**Q15. Reusable vs. bespoke.** `[B↑]`

- A: "New team members often build something custom from scratch."
- B: "Teams commonly use templates and configuration guides for AI tools."

### D7 Value integration (added 2026-04-24, see Framework v1.2)

**Q16. AI absence test.** `[B↑]`

- A: "AI service outages mostly impact our productivity, and at most some product features."
- B: "Our product would stop working if AI-services were impacted."

**Q17. AI in the product shape.** `[A↑]`

- A: "Our product line includes offerings that didn't exist before AI made them possible."
- B: "AI has improved our current products, or has the potential to improve our products."

## Remaining open concerns

1. **Q3 on "new hire" framing** — the question is about practitioner legibility. Assumes the org has hired recently. Most orgs do. If the respondent is at a very small/static org, the framing still works because "can someone explain the reasoning" is the underlying test.
2. **Q10 ChatGPT Plus example** — very concrete but ties to a specific product. Works in 2026; risk of dating in a year. Alternative: "their own AI subscription."
3. **Q11 invisibility paradox risk** — still a known edge case. An L1 org with no protocols at all might pick "A" (nothing to chase because nothing exists). Mitigation: Q9 + Q10 catch the L1 state via visibility failure; combined scores should resolve correctly. Worth validating when we run real responses.
4. **Q12 "knows the domain cold"** — idiomatic. Might feel American English. Alternatives: "has deep experience" or "deeply familiar with the domain".
