# Rao — Constructing the Evil Twin of AI

## Summary
Develops the framing of protocols as devices that turn *smooth* behavior spaces (open, unbuilt terrain) into *striated* behavior spaces (systems of roads). Uses Deleuze and Guattari's smooth-vs-striated distinction to characterise what protocols do: they don't just regulate behavior, they reshape the topology of what's possible. AI's "evil twin" is the protocol-laden infrastructure that emerges around it — what makes AI legible and governable, not the model itself.

## Why it matters for the CMM
Two load-bearing contributions to the framework:

1. **Protocol vs. policy distinction.** This is the cleanest source for distinguishing protocols (which reshape what's possible) from policies (which prohibit specific actions). Section 2 of the litepaper depends on this distinction; the assessment's hierarchical scoring inherits it (the `Driver` axis is explicitly Chosen-vs-Constrained, not just rule-presence).
2. **Smooth-to-striated as a maturity progression.** The CMM levels 1 → 5 can be read as smooth behavior (L1 Shadow — open terrain, individual paths) progressively striating into shared infrastructure (L4–L5 — sector-wide protocol topology). Rao's framing is the philosophical scaffolding under the model.

## Claims citable from this observation
- "To borrow a pair of terms from the postmodernists, protocols turn smooth behavior spaces (such as open, unbuilt terrain) into striated behavior spaces (such as a system of roads)." (verbatim, Rao)
- AI's "evil twin" is the protocol layer that makes it legible and governable — not the model itself.
