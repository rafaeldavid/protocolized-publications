# Protocols for Business SIG — Finding Fault Lines within the Firm

## Summary
The Protocols for Business Special Interest Group's foundational paper. Argues that persistent organizational problems and persistent points of failure are revealed under stress — not through close surface inspection but through the accumulated pressure that forces underlying structure to express itself. Frames the firm's protocol layer (the encoded rules governing coordination) as analogous to a geologic fault system: pressure concentrates and trade-offs stabilise along specific lines.

Also the source of the **management subsumption thesis**: management practice as a whole is what is "necessary and effective in context"; protocols, when encoded in software, narrow that to "what is authorized and auditable" — the gap between practiced management and protocol-encoded management is where AI deployment fails most visibly.

## Why it matters for the CMM
Four load-bearing contributions:

1. **Site-naming reference** for protocolized.dev — "fault lines" is the namesake for the hero image (`finding-fault-lines.jpg`) used across all six pages.
2. **Bottleneck-thinking foundation.** "The slowest constraint sets pace for the entire system" — the governing-clock concept. This justifies the assessment's bottleneck-scoring approach: the effective maturity level is the highest-stakes, weakest-governed function.
3. **Practiced vs. encoded management.** The litepaper's Level 2 "policy/practice gap" failure mode is sourced from this paper. Air Canada's chatbot case is the canonical example of what happens when AI enforces the encoded version uniformly while the practiced version had contextual exceptions.
4. **Air Canada citation.** The verbatim Air Canada framing in the case-studies page (`#chatbot-liability` card and the litepaper) traces to this paper's treatment of *Moffatt v. Air Canada*.

## Claims citable from this observation
- "In geology, fault lines are not identified by close surface inspection. They are discovered when accumulated stress forces the underlying structure to express itself.
