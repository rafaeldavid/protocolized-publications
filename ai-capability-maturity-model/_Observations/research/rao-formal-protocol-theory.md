# Rao — What is Formal Protocol Theory? (2025)

## Summary
Venkatesh Rao's working note framing **Formal Protocol Theory (FPT)** as a developing analytical project: the construction of rigorous tools for studying coordination mechanisms across scales. The piece outlines a session structure (including an upcoming session on Witsenhausen's counterexample as a touchstone for impossibility and symmetry results in distributed control) and positions FPT as analytical methodology rather than as policy or compliance framework. Notable: the document does *not* state an explicit protocol-vs-policy distinction — that contrast is better sourced from Entry 20 (Protocol Reader) and Entry 24 (Evil Twin).

## Why it matters for the CMM
Section 2 of the litepaper uses FPT as the methodological grounding for *why* the CMM is protocol-framed rather than compliance-framed. Three contributions:

1. **Methodological precision.** FPT establishes that protocol analysis is its own analytical discipline — it is not a softer version of policy or governance. The CMM inherits this framing: maturity is measured by protocol structure, not by rule compliance.
2. **Witsenhausen as a marker for distributed-coordination hardness.** The Witsenhausen counterexample reference is a load-bearing signal that distributed AI authority (which the model addresses at Levels 3+) is genuinely structurally hard, not merely organizationally inconvenient.
3. **Rigorous-but-developing.** FPT is explicitly an emerging field; citing it accurately means citing it as methodology-in-development, not as a finished theory.
