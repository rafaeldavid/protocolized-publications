# Humphrey — Managing the Software Process (1989)

## Summary
Watts S. Humphrey's 1989 book established the original Software Engineering Institute Capability Maturity Model (SEI CMM). Five levels: **Initial** (ad hoc), **Repeatable** (some repeatability), **Defined** (documented), **Managed** (measured), **Optimizing** (continuous improvement). Level 1's defining characteristic is that success depends on individual heroics, not replicable process. Originated at SEI, Carnegie Mellon University, commissioned by the US Department of Defense (1986). CMMI (2002) superseded the original CMM by integrating multiple frameworks; CMMI is currently maintained by ISACA.

## Why it matters for the CMM
The intellectual lineage this AI Capability Maturity Model openly inherits. Three contributions:

1. **Five-level architecture.** The CMM's five-level structure is a direct adaptation of Humphrey's. Naming the lineage explicitly is what makes the model legible to enterprise audiences who already know SEI CMM, CMMI, or ISO/IEC 15504.
2. **"Individual heroics" as the canonical Level 1.** Humphrey's framing of Level 1 — success that depends on talented individuals rather than repeatable process — is exactly the Shadow level pattern in the AI CMM. The diagnosis is unchanged across forty years; only the medium (AI use vs. software development) is new.
3. **Maturity ≠ tool adoption.** Humphrey's central claim was that mature organizations have *defined process*, not better tools. This is the load-bearing premise the AI CMM inherits and applies to AI tooling specifically.
