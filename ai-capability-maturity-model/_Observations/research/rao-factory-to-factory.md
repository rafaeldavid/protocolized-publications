# Rao — Have Your Factory Call My Factory

## Summary
Working note describing what production looks like when each participant has built their own AI-driven workflow ("factory") and the coordination problem reduces to handing intermediate artifacts between factories. Uses a personal example: the author orchestrates a writing pipeline at the shell level — "like a factory floor supervisor" — and the handoff with a collaborator is a Dropbox folder plus a metadata server, not a shared editing surface.

## Why it matters for the CMM
Three contributions:

1. **The F2F (factory-to-factory) coordination pattern.** This is the structural pattern Level 4 anticipates at sector scale: not "one shared AI tool" but "many bespoke AI-enabled factories whose coordination problem is the handoff format." The intermediate artifact — not the AI itself — is what circulates.
2. **Guild archetype anchor.** The Guild archetype is the pre-organizational version of the F2F pattern — sophistication concentrated at individual practitioners running personal factories without yet a sector-level handoff protocol. The Guild's growth direction (toward Architect-in-domain) is the move from personal factory to platform.
3. **Domain knowledge as the limiting input.** The author's framing — "I haven't touched a line of content text… I watch the action entirely at the shell level" — is the L3 maturity claim: AI provides commodity execution; domain knowledge is the scarce resource. The Level 3 "Designed" page on the levels site references this framing directly.

## Claims citable from this observation
- "So far I haven't touched a line of content text, and haven't even looked at any code. I watch the action entirely at the shell level, like a factory floor supervisor." (verbatim, Rao)
- "The handoff point between us is a shared Dropbox folder plus a 'manuscript transmittal' server she's set up for metadata." (verbatim, Rao)
- The F2F pattern: AI-enabled factories coordinate via intermediate artifacts, not via shared tools.
