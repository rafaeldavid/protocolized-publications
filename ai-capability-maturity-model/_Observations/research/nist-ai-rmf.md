# NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (January 2023)

## Summary
The National Institute of Standards and Technology's AI Risk Management Framework, published as NIST AI 100-1 on **January 26, 2023**. Voluntary and non-sector-specific. Organized around four core functions: **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage**. Although voluntary, the framework is referenced in proposed federal regulations and in sector guidance from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) and the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). A Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) was released in 2024 as a GenAI-specific supplement. Remains the primary US government AI risk guidance as of March 2026.

## Why it matters for the CMM
US enterprise governance reference; statutory complement to the EU AI Act in the global compliance landscape addressed in Section 5. Three contributions:

1. **Govern / Map / Measure / Manage maps cleanly to L3+ protocol design.** The four functions correspond to the protocol-design requirements that distinguish Level 3 from Level 2: explicit governance ownership, risk mapping, measurement, and managed mitigation.
2. **Voluntary-but-referenced status is the typical US pattern.** Unlike the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF is not directly enforceable — but its incorporation into OCC and HHS guidance turns it into a *de facto* standard for regulated US sectors. This is the structural counterpoint to Europe's statutory approach.
3. **GenAI Profile (600-1) shows the framework adapts to model class.** Confirms NIST RMF treats AI risk as protocol-shaped (function-level requirements) rather than as model-specific compliance, consistent with the CMM's protocol framing.
