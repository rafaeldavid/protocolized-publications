# Legal hallucination sanctions (2023–2026)

## Summary
A growing body of court-record and bar-association evidence documenting the legal-profession failure mode of unsanctioned AI use. As of early 2026, more than 550 publicly reported hallucination cases have been tracked across courts, with 712 judicial decisions worldwide noting AI-generated misinformation. The largest single sanction to date — $86,000 — was levied in *ByoPlanet v. Johansson*. The American Bar Association's Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) requires lawyers to have a "reasonable understanding" of AI capabilities and to verify all output. Earliest canonical case: *Mata v. Avianca* (2023), in which counsel filed a brief citing fabricated case law generated by ChatGPT.

## Why it matters for the CMM
Canonical Level-1 case at scale — distinct from Samsung in that the failure mode is *output quality*, not *data exfiltration*. Three contributions:

1. **Cross-firm shadow AI evidence.** 550+ tracked cases shows the failure mode is structural, not anecdotal. This anchors the litepaper's claim that Level 1 is the modal state, not an outlier.
2. **Regulatory forcing function within a profession.** ABA Formal Opinion 512 turns "I didn't know it could do that" into a defensible verification obligation — the legal profession's analog of the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations.
3. **Concrete sanction figure.** The $86K ByoPlanet number is a usable empirical anchor for "Level 1 has direct financial cost," distinct from breach-cost averages.
