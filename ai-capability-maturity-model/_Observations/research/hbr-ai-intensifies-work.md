# Ranganathan & Ye — AI Doesn't Reduce Work, It Intensifies It (HBR, February 2026)

## Summary
Aruna Ranganathan and Xingqi Maggie Ye argue in HBR that AI adoption does not reduce work in surveyed organizations — it intensifies it. Workers expand their task portfolios, multitask more aggressively, and work beyond normal hours after AI adoption. The intensification appears to be largely intrinsically motivated: workers do this to themselves, not in response to direct managerial pressure. The paper is a counterpoint to vendor narratives of "AI saves time" by showing the time gains being absorbed back into expanded scope at the individual level.

## Why it matters for the CMM
Empirical evidence for the L3/L1 mismatch — the model's claim that individual-level adoption (often L1) without an organizational protocol layer (L3+) produces intensification rather than reallocation. Three contributions:

1. **Intensification as the symptom of a missing protocol layer.** When AI is adopted without a workflow-design protocol, the organizational system has no mechanism to absorb time savings; it routes them back into individual scope.
2. **Intrinsic motivation finding raises the bar.** Workers driving their own intensification means the failure cannot be diagnosed as managerial coercion — it is structural, baked into the absence of protocol-level capacity allocation.
3. **Direct counterevidence to the vendor "AI saves time" narrative.** Useful in Section 5 of the litepaper for distinguishing individual productivity claims (true) from organizational value capture (false absent protocol design).
