# Source References — Terraforming the Org

## Primary sources for the industrial terraforming metaphor

### 1. Atack, Bateman & Weiss (1980)
**Citation:** Atack, J., Bateman, F., & Weiss, T. (1980). The regional diffusion and adoption of the steam engine in American manufacturing. *Journal of Economic History*, 40(2), 281–308.

**Key arguments:**
- Steam engine adoption followed a logistic (S-curve) diffusion pattern, not linear growth
- Regional variation was driven by coal price and proximity to water power — areas near cheap water power were slower to adopt steam
- By 1870, steam had become the dominant motive power in U.S. manufacturing, particularly inland

**Relevance to terraforming thesis:** Geographic liberation — the steam engine freed factories from river constraints, allowing inland expansion. Regional diffusion data shows the S-curve of a true paradigm shift, not incremental improvement.

### 2. Devine (1983)
**Citation:** Devine, W. D., Jr. (1983). From shafts to wires: Historical perspective on electrification. *Journal of Economic History*, 43(2), 347–372.

**Key arguments:**
- Electricity's primary value was not energy savings but *form value*: spatial, temporal, and scale precision of power delivery unavailable from steam
- First-phase electrification (1890s–1910s): replace central steam engine with electric motor, keep line-shaft architecture — minimal productivity gain
- Second-phase electrification (1910s–1920s): *unit drive* — one motor per machine, factory floor completely redesigned — manufacturing output per man-hour growth jumps from 1.3%/yr to 3.1%/yr
- "A fundamental change in viewpoint preceded and accompanied exploitation"

**Relevance to terraforming thesis:** The bolt-on/redesign distinction is the core analogy for AI levels 1–2 (replace the shaft with electric motor = use ChatGPT the way you used Google) vs. L3+ (unit drive = redesign workflows around AI capability).

### 3. Theorizing Protocolization I: New Nature
**URL:** https://protocolized.summerofprotocols.com/p/theorizing-protocolization-i-new

**Key arguments:**
- Protocols, when fully adopted, become the environment itself — invisible infrastructure rather than a tool
- "New Nature": the environment of action transforms so completely that the protocol is no longer noticed as a protocol
- This is the end-state of any successful paradigm adoption

**Relevance to terraforming thesis:** Provides the conceptual anchor for what "terraforming" means at the organizational level — AI doesn't just change how work gets done, it becomes the medium in which work happens. The organization that has completed the transition no longer "uses AI"; it operates in an AI-native environment.
