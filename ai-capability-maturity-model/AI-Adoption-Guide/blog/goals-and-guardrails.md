# Goals and Guardrails

*Agentic engineering is converging on two primitives. The historical precedent — and the change in work it implies — is sharper than the tooling discourse suggests.*

A pattern is converging across agentic engineering tooling, and it is worth naming. On 12 May 2026 Anthropic shipped Claude Code 2.1.139 with a `/goal` command: a completion condition specified upfront — *all tests passing*, *the vulnerability patched* — after which the agent iterates plan / act / test / review across many turns until the goal is met or the budget is exhausted. OpenClaw, Hermes, and Codex have adopted variants of the same primitive within weeks. A month earlier, OpenAI published the production security architecture for Codex: container-based sandboxes that define what the agent technically *can* do, approval workflows that decide *when it must stop and ask* before crossing those boundaries, network policies and agent-native telemetry around the whole stack. The shape both announcements share is identical. Agentic engineering is converging on **goals + guardrails**.

The NPC Memo's recent essays push the same idea to its enterprise conclusion. *Infrastructure of Firm Control* argues that once a firm executes faster than its board can supervise, governance has to be embedded into technical constraints — interlocks, circuit breakers, rate limits, fail-safe defaults — that run at machine speed. The phrase the essay returns to is **constitutional software**: the firm's commitments instantiated as executable limits within the system itself, not as policies waiting for human enforcement. Its companion piece, *Most Management Today Isn't Leadership*, extends the argument: the manager's job migrates from supervising people to designing and maintaining the protocols that encode the organization's theory of responsibility.

Put the three together and the picture is clear. The unit of human work shifts from *doing the task* to *specifying the goal and bounding the envelope*. The agent does the doing. The system enforces what the agent cannot do regardless of what the goal asks for. The human reviews the result and the protocol drift.

This is not the first time work has restructured around the goal-plus-envelope split. The clearest parallel is **fly-by-wire envelope protection in commercial aviation**, mature by the early 1990s with the Airbus A320. The captain's job changed irreversibly. Where the prior generation of pilots manipulated control surfaces continuously, the A320 pilot specifies *intent* — a waypoint, an altitude, a descent rate — and the flight envelope protection system refuses any input that would enter an unrecoverable state regardless of the captain's command. Stall protection cannot be overridden in normal law. Bank-angle limits cannot be overridden. The system encodes what the airframe is constitutionally incapable of doing, and the pilot operates inside that envelope.

The work changed because the *unit* changed. The pilot's contribution moved up the stack from actuator-level control to goal-level navigation and exception handling — the same migration agentic engineering is now executing for the knowledge worker. The pattern is durable: once goal-specification and constitutional constraints can be made executable in a system, the layer above them stops being supervised and starts being directed. Codex's sandbox is the bank-angle limit. Claude's `/goal` is the waypoint. The manager's job is to set the destination and design the envelope.
