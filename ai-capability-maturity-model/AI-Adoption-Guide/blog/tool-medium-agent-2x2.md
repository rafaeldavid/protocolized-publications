# The Tool / Medium × In-Hand / Agent 2×2

*A classification matrix for technology — companion analytical frame to* When the Technology Is the Terrain.

## The two orthogonal axes

- **Tool ↔ Medium** answers: *how much of the surrounding environment has to reorganize before the technology pays off?* A tool is dropped in (environment unchanged). A medium is a substrate (everything reorganizes around it).
- **In-Hand ↔ Agent** answers: *who supplies the intentionality?* In-Hand technologies are wielded — the human is the agent. Agent technologies pursue goals on their own — the artifact has its own perception–action loop.

The two questions are independent. Their cross-product gives four quadrants.

## How to read the matrix

- **The diagonal tells the story.** *Implement* and *Inhabited Terrain* are the two extremes. The diagonal from hammer to agentic AI is the full arc of technological history — from artifact-the-human-wields-in-an-unchanged-world to substrate-that-hosts-autonomous-actors-and-rewrites-everything.
- **The off-diagonal quadrants are stable historical categories.** *Bounded Helper* has been densely populated for millennia (sheepdog → autopilot → Roomba). *Substrate* has been densely populated since writing emerged. Both have mature governance disciplines.
- **The Inhabited Terrain quadrant is the unstable one.** Every historical occupant required a century-plus of evolving constitutional infrastructure to become governable. The bet behind protocols-for-business / constitutional-software is that AI's version can be compressed into a release cycle rather than a century.
- **AI's strangeness is that it can occupy any of the four boxes depending on deployment posture.** A single LLM call inside a fixed app is an *Implement*. An agent with a defined scope (Dependabot-style) is a *Bounded Helper*. A natural-language workflow that reshapes a team's coordination is a *Substrate*. An organization built around continuous agentic production is an *Inhabited Terrain*. The same underlying model crosses all four; the box depends on how the firm deploys it.
- **The two-track Cultivated/Governed prescription is the unique response shape for the Inhabited Terrain quadrant.** Cultivated handles the *substrate* axis (trace-generation in the new medium); Governed handles the *agent* axis (envelope-design for autonomous actors). Neither track alone has historical precedent for the combined task — the Inhabited Terrain quadrant is the first general-purpose technology asking humanity to build the two disciplines simultaneously, on the same timescale, inside the same firm.

## Why "Agent" and not the other candidates for the third pole

- **Protocol** — collapses into Medium at scale (TCP/IP, the shipping-container system, traffic law all become substrates the moment they coordinate enough actors). Not orthogonal.
- **Network** — same problem; a sufficiently dense network *is* a medium.
- **Practice / skill** — describes the human side of the relation, not a property of the technology. Belongs on a separate axis (operator skill), not as a third vertex.
- **Capability / force** (fire, fission, electricity-as-raw-energy) — describes a precursor state before classification, not a distinct pole. Fire becomes tool, medium, or agent depending on how it's deployed.

Agent earns the third-pole spot because it answers a genuinely different question (*whose action is this?*) and because it is uniquely load-bearing for AI — the first general-purpose technology engineered to occupy the Medium + Agent quadrant from day one.

## The diagnostic move

For any new technology, ask three orthogonal questions:

- **Tool question:** what does this replace? (the hand-saw question)
- **Medium question:** what would have to rearrange for this to pay off? (the line-shaft question)
- **Agent question:** what does this do on its own, and what must it be told it cannot do? (the envelope question)

A technology that scores high on only the first is a tool. A technology that scores high on the first two is a medium-class technology being deployed as a tool — the historical lag pattern (electrification, IT, the web). A technology that scores high on all three is the new thing — and AI is the first one humanity has built at general-purpose scale.
