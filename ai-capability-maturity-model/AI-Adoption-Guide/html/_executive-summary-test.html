Executive summary · For operations leaders

Durable AI adoption: what your program needs from you

Your organization is mid-deployment. Adoption is a different track: what your teams discover once the tools are in their hands. That is where competitive position is now being set.

Five claims to act on
  1. Deployment is half the job. Tools, policy, and approved access produce a record of activity. Adoption is what your teams build on top of that: markdown skills, prompt templates, shared notebooks. Most of it is invisible to your dashboards today.
  2. Count protocol precision, not users. Maturity is how tightly AI behavior is governed across your work, at growing scope. The weakest function sets the organization's level. Klarna reports 90% staff use of AI, paired with generic output and unmanaged exposure.
  3. Cultivation feeds governance. The protocols worth formalizing are the ones domain experts have already proved by running them. Policy without that stream calcifies into theater, and you get the adoption-rate-up, value-flat trap.
  4. The same four failure modes recur. Standardizing before kits prove value. Commitments outpacing comprehension. AI accelerating one step faster than the next. Agent permissions outpacing agent judgment. Each has a known mitigation. None of them are tool choices.
  5. Six work categories need named owners now. Kit Harvesting, Evals, Forward Deployment, Platform Operations, Risk Stewardship, Context Stabilization. If you cannot name an owner for each today, you are running a tools program, not yet an adoption program.
Why this matters

We have been here before. Electricity in 1908. Containers in 1956. EDI in 1988. The firms that won were not first to install. They were first to redraw work around the new unit.

Tool access has converged. Protocol precision has not. That is the gap your team is working in, and it needs your visible backing to close.

This program has my backing. Three things I want us to do before quarter-end:

  1. Find what already works in practice before standardizing it.
  2. Name an owner for each of the six work categories above. Send me the list.
