# Play, Kit, Practice: A Hands-On Workshop for Homebrew AI Tools

*Workshop proposal — a keynote and three working sessions across the symposium's two workshop days, plus async co-working on Discord.*

Most teams "adopt AI" by buying fragile, often unreliable AI software and waiting for a productivity jump that never shows up in the numbers.

The missing productivity is the signature of the current phase of AI adoption: **Kits**. Every general-purpose technology — the steam engine, the Model T, the PC — passes through a kit phase: loose components people tinker with, repurpose, and master by doing, long before anyone builds the firm-scale machinery that turns that tinkering into measured output. Farmers ran corn shellers and water pumps off jacked-up Model Ts for years before Ford watched them and built the tractor. The work that matters is happening in mostly undocumented, local experiments, and the organizations that pull ahead are the ones that recognize the stage they are in and build the map to climb out of it.

This workshop is the hands-on answer. Over the symposium's two workshop days, participants go from ad-hoc AI use to building their first Kit — reusable workflows that individuals and teams can run repeatedly and reliably — and then learn how to approach scaling those kits in a sustainable way. Every level is touched by hand: participants **Play**, build a **Kit**, and prepare the path to make it a **Practice**.

The workshop is aimed at individuals from all backgrounds looking to go from 0 to 1 on AI usage. No AI-engineering background is required, only a license to Claude Code or OpenAI's Codex, a GitHub account, and a short list of projects they would like to tackle.

The workshop runs two tracks: beginner and intermediate. Beginners, who have limited experience beyond AI chatbots, are guided through a structured case study built around a public HTML-based application. Intermediate participants, already familiar with concepts such as MCP and Skills, develop a custom project — for example, an agent in the spirit of the Protocol Institute's C3PO — to demo to the group.

**Learning outcome:** by the end, each participant has (1) at least one working Kit they built and can re-run, and (2) a working understanding of the five-level AI Capability Maturity Model and where their own work sits on it.

**Maximum participants:** 10.

**Format:** one keynote and three working sessions across the two workshop days, plus async co-working on Discord (kit-building help, debugging, sharing what people make). Runs on a coding agent — Claude Code or OpenAI Codex — plus a GitHub account. Built on the AI Adoption Guide and its embedded diagnostic at [ai.protocolized.dev](https://ai.protocolized.dev/).

## Keynote — The Homebrew Era of AI

In 1975 the Homebrew Computer Club was a room of hobbyists soldering kits no one had a use for yet; a few years later those kits were Apple, and an industry. AI is in its homebrew era now — a general-purpose technology in its kit phase, where the real skill is being built in local, undocumented experiments rather than in the productivity statistics. This keynote makes the case that AI adoption is a maturity problem, not a tooling one; introduces the five-level AI Capability Maturity Model as the map from homebrew tinkering to durable practice; and, at a symposium about protocols as the new nature, frames AI-adoption friction as a protocol-design problem. It sets up the workshop's arc — Play, Kit, Practice — as the first rungs of that climb.

## Session 1 — Freeform Play and the Context Hub (basic)

Set up your environment, assemble a basic context hub for your project — the small bundle of goals, references, and conventions the AI works from — and deploy your first disposable application. Beginners follow a guided HTML case study; intermediate participants stand up the scaffolding for their custom project.

## Session 2 — Kit Development (intermediate)

Convert your disposable application into a reusable Kit: a workflow you, or a teammate, can run repeatedly and reliably, with the context, prompts, and steps encoded rather than retyped. Beginners harden the case-study app into a repeatable kit; intermediate participants build out their custom project (an agent, skill, or MCP-connected tool).

## Session 3 — Kit Showcase (intermediate)

Demo day. Each participant shows the Kit they built and sketches how they would scale it — the path from a personal kit toward a team Practice — followed by a short clinic on what tends to break at the next maturity level and a set of open problems.
